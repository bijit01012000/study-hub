    How to Approach Any LLD Problem

    9 sections · 688 words

    Framework · LLD Core Concepts · Concept 09 of 09

    The Idea

    The same order, every time.

    The Five Steps

    1. Clarify the ask: 2-3 sentences of scope, out loud, before any code.
    2. Pull out the nouns: they're your classes.
    3. Pull out the verbs: they're your methods.
    4. Draw the relationships: association, aggregation, composition, inheritance.
    5. Walk one real scenario through the diagram, out loud.

    Common First-Move Mistakes

    • Jumping to a pattern before you know what's actually variable.
    • Writing code before drawing a single box.

    Quick Check

    If you can't name the 3-4 core classes in the first 2 minutes, you skipped step 1.

    How It's Built

    The Shape: Steps 1-2

    "Design a parking lot: vehicles of different sizes, spots of
different sizes, track availability, compute a fee on exit."

Nouns -> classes:
    Vehicle, Spot, ParkingLot, Ticket, FeeStrategy

Verbs -> methods:
    park(vehicle), unpark(ticket), fee(ticket), isAvailable(spot)

    The Shape: Steps 4-5

    ParkingLot --keeps many--> Spot
ParkingLot --issues-->     Ticket
Ticket     --references--> Vehicle
ParkingLot --has one-->    FeeStrategy

Scenario: a Sedan parks in a Spot, a Ticket is issued. On exit,
fee(ticket) asks FeeStrategy, ParkingLot marks the Spot free.

    The Same Shape, Drawn

    ParkingLot
    - fee: FeeStrategy
    + park(v): Ticket
    + fee(t): int

    Spend Your 40 Minutes (A Budget)

    1. First 10 min: clarify, nouns/verbs, draw the boxes. Don't write a line of code yet.
    2. Next 20 min: implement the 2-3 core methods, narrating each design choice out loud.
    3. Last 10 min: name 2 follow-ups yourself before they ask: it signals you know the edges.

    In The Interview

    "Before I write anything: the nouns are Vehicle, Spot, ParkingLot, Ticket, and something that varies: the fee rule. The verbs are park, unpark, fee. Let me sketch how they relate, then walk a Sedan through it end to end."

    How It's Different

    The Confusion Pairs

    A framework vs a fixed checklist. The five steps are an order, not a script to recite. Skip step 2 if the nouns are obvious from the prompt.

    This approach vs jumping to patterns. Patterns and SOLID are tools you reach for once you're inside the design, not the starting point.

    Nouns to classes vs a class per noun. Not every noun becomes a class. "Size" is usually a field or enum, not a class of its own.

    One scenario vs every scenario. Walking one clean path through your diagram is usually enough to prove it holds together. Interviewers rarely need every edge case walked live.

    They Say: "go ahead, start designing"

    • With it: 90 seconds of nouns/verbs/relationships out loud, then a clean diagram they watch you build.
    • Without: silence, then a diagram that changes shape three times as you second-guess it live.

    Watch Out For

    • Silence while you think reads worse than thinking out loud, even half-formed. Narrate the extraction as you do it.
    • Don't ask "should I use a pattern here" as your first question. Find the class boundaries first; patterns fall out of them.
    • A diagram with 15 classes for a 40-minute problem is over-scoped. Merge the ones that carry no real behaviour.
    • If two classes' responsibilities keep overlapping as you draw, that's SRP telling you the boundary is in the wrong place, mid-design.
    • End with "any questions on this design" instead of your last line of code. Invite the pressure instead of waiting for it.
