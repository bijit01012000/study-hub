    Cost, latency and scalability

    1 sections · 436 words

    Module 12 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Know what your system costs and where its time goes.

    This module tests whether a candidate has actually opened the bill. Cost fluency is rare and easy to fake with one sentence that survives a first pass and collapses under the second question: which requests drove the spend, what did it do to quality, and how do you know. The follow-ups chase attribution and measurement, not vocabulary.

    Inside this module: Token economics · Caching · Routing · Latency decomposition

    Concept checkpoints

    Q12.1 — Walk me through where your token bill actually comes from.

    What it really tests: Whether spend is decomposed into input versus output tokens, fixed context and model tier, not one number.

    Q12.2 — How do you know what a request, a user or a feature actually costs?

    What it really tests: Whether real attribution exists, tagging and per-call logs, or only a single dashboard total.

    Q12.3 — Talk me through prompt caching versus semantic caching, and where each breaks.

    What it really tests: Whether these are understood as different mechanisms with different failure modes, not one word for caching.

    Q12.4 — How do you decide when to route to a small model versus a large one?

    What it really tests: Whether routing is a measured policy tied to task difficulty and checked accuracy, not a blanket cheap versus costly call.

    Q12.5 — You add streaming to a slow chat interface. What actually changes, and what doesn't?

    What it really tests: Whether time to first token is separated from total completion time, and which levers move which.

    Q12.6 — Someone suggests batching your interactive chat calls the way you batch overnight jobs. Your answer?

    What it really tests: Whether batching is understood as a delay for discount trade specific to asynchronous work, not a general cost lever.

    Q12.7 — Your document search feature has a high p95 latency. How do you find where the time goes?

    What it really tests: Whether the call chain gets traced stage by stage, or the candidate assumes which part is slow.

    Q12.8 — Cut your copilot's cost without users noticing a quality drop. Walk me through it.

    What it really tests: Whether there is a measured, multi lever plan with a quality check, or one obvious lever called done.

    Q12.9 — DRILL: produce a costed reduction plan for a slow, expensive document processing pipeline.

    What it really tests: Whether cost attribution, latency decomposition and lever choice combine into one plan with named trade-offs.
