    Design an Ad Click Aggregator

    3 sections · 3,159 words

    Three levels: Level 1 — The Shape, Level 2 — The Defence, Level 3 — The Edges.

    Level 1 — The Shape (Foundation)

    Pacing: 3 min ask · 5 min trace flow · 7 min read why.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Ingest clicks tagged campaign_id + event-time
    • Count clicks + unique clickers, per campaign/window

    Non-Functional

    • Sub-minute-ish aggregate latency ← forces streaming, not batch
    • Late data can't block output forever ← forces a watermark

    Agree These First

    100,000 campaigns · ~5,000 avg clicks/campaign/day · 1-min windows, 2-min watermark grace. Config and fraud modelling are someone else's system. Retries aren't prevented — dedup handles them.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Click Event Source — Ad-serving client · redirect pixel
    • Ingestion Log — Partitioned by campaign_id — e.g. Kafka
    • (2) Stream Aggregator — Windowed count + HLL sketch, per campaign; campaign-partitioned; click events; 17,361 /s peak
    • Aggregate Store — Queryable time-series output, per campaign per window

    Data model: window_counts campaign_id+window PK → count, unique_hll.

    Line = flow type: COUNT · click → aggregate; QUERY · store → dashboard.

    Say It Like This

    "This is a stream I can't fully store and can't fully wait on. I'd aggregate in the stream — windowed counts per campaign, not a nightly batch — because budget-pacing can't wait hours. A raw counter can't answer 'clicks in the last minute', so counting needs a time axis. And for unique clickers I'd reach for an HLL sketch, not a set of IDs — bounded memory, however big a campaign gets."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) Stream vs. batch Aggregate in the stream — budget-pacing can't wait hours Nightly batch → overspend nobody catches in time
    (6) Counting needs a time axis Windowed state, per campaign per minute — a real time axis One counter → a raw total, write-contended, no time axis
    (13) Bounded, not forever Cold storage keeps 7 days — enough to reconcile, not an archive Store every click forever → on-demand counting re-scans history forever

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Clicks 100,000 campaigns × 5,000 avg clicks/day 500,000,000/day
    Avg rate 500,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 5,787 /s
    Peak (×3) peaks 3x the daily average 17,361 /s
    HLL sketch p=14 · 16,384 regs × 6 bits — ~0.81% error 12,288 B
    In-flight state 300,000 × 12 KB dense HLL — the ceiling 3.69 GB

    Round hard, out loud. 500 million clicks a day sounds enormous; the number that actually matters is 3.69 GB — what you provision for in-flight state. It is the dense-sketch ceiling; most windows are sparse and far smaller. Provision the ceiling, not the average.

    Before You Draw Anything — The Four Moves

    1. Clarify, don't assume. Exact count or approximate is fine? Real-time or near-real-time? Is fraud filtering in scope at all? — Two minutes of questions buys the whole design.
    2. Agree the scale out loud. "100,000 campaigns, 500 million clicks a day, peaking to 17,361 a second." Now every choice has a reason. — An unstated number is an undefendable design.
    3. Draw the happy path only. Client → ingestion log → stream aggregator → aggregate store. Get agreement before adding anything. — Boxes you add before agreement are boxes you'll defend alone.
    4. Name one trade-off unprompted. "I'd use an HLL sketch for unique counts, not an exact set — bounded memory over exact precision." — This is the single move that separates L1 from a junior answer.

    What L1 Is Testing

    • Do you split "count" from "unique count" as two problems?
    • Do you reach for streaming before being told the latency need?
    • Did you ask about late data before drawing anything?

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Why aggregate in the stream instead of a nightly batch report? (ref 3 — tests: near-real-time budget-pacing — a batch delay risks unrecoverable overspend)
    2. Why can't a simple incrementing counter give you "the count"? (refs 3,6 — tests: do you reach for a time axis, not just a bigger number)
    3. Why not store every click forever and count on demand? (ref 13 — tests: bounded-retention instinct, not endless rescanning)

    At L1 you are being judged on one thing: can you produce a coherent shape and defend three choices inside it. Depth is page 2 — do not reach for it here. Ghosted boxes are what arrives next; you should be able to guess two of them.

    Level 2 — The Defence (Intermediate)

    Pacing: 5 min ask · 15 min trace flow · 10 min read why · 30 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Ingest clicks tagged campaign_id + event-time
    • Count clicks + unique clickers, per campaign/window
    • Tolerate late, out-of-order clicks, bounded
    • Dedup retried pings — idempotent on click_id

    Non-Functional

    • Sub-minute-ish aggregate latency ← forces streaming, not batch
    • Late data can't block output forever ← forces a watermark
    • Bounded memory per campaign ← forces HLL over exact sets
    • Idempotent counting under retries ← forces click_id dedup

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Campaign config / budgets — elsewhere
    • Deep fraud modelling — basic filter only
    • Ad serving / creative selection

    Agree These First

    100,000 campaigns · ~5,000 avg clicks/campaign/day · 1-min windows, 2-min watermark grace. Config and fraud modelling are someone else's system. Retries aren't prevented — dedup handles them.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Click Event Source — Ad-serving client · redirect pixel
    • Ingestion Log — Partitioned by campaign_id — e.g. Kafka
    • (3) Stream Aggregator — Windowed count + HLL sketch, per campaign; campaign-partitioned; click events; peak 17,361 clicks/s
    • (5) Windowed State Store — RocksDB-backed — window counters + HLL sketches
    • (6) Watermark Tracker — emits when the watermark passes; "No more late data before T" per window
    • (7) Dedup Cache — click_id idempotency · TTL ~5 min; 300,000 entries ~ 3.69 GB
    • Late-Data Side Output — Arrived after the watermark passed
    • Aggregate Store — Queryable time-series output, per campaign per window
    • Dashboard / Advertiser Query — Reads from Aggregate Store

    Data model:

    • window_counts campaign_id+window PK · count · unique_hll · emitted_at
    • seen_click_ids click_id PK · TTL ~5 min — dedup only

    Line = flow type: COUNT · click → aggregate; QUERY · store → dashboard; CHECK · watermark / dedup.

    Say It Like This

    "This is a stream I can't fully store and can't fully wait on. I'd aggregate in the stream — windowed counts per campaign, not a nightly batch — because budget-pacing can't wait hours. A raw counter can't answer 'clicks in the last minute', so counting needs a time axis. And for unique clickers I'd reach for an HLL sketch, not a set of IDs — bounded memory, however big a campaign gets."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) Stream vs. batch Aggregate in the stream — budget-pacing can't wait hours Nightly batch → overspend nobody catches in time
    (6) Counting needs a time axis Windowed state, per campaign per minute — a real time axis One counter → a raw total, write-contended, no time axis
    (13) Bounded, not forever Cold storage keeps 7 days — enough to reconcile, not an archive Store every click forever → on-demand counting re-scans history forever
    (5) Watermark deadline An explicit deadline — "no more late data before T" Wait forever for stragglers → a window never closes
    (6) HLL vs. exact set Fixed ~12 KB sketch — memory never grows, ~1% error Exact click_id set → 320 MB for one hot campaign
    (7) Dedup cache click_id idempotency, TTL ~5 min — a retry isn't recounted No dedup → a mobile-network retry silently double-counts

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Clicks 100,000 campaigns × 5,000 avg clicks/day 500,000,000/day
    Avg rate 500,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 5,787 /s
    Peak (×3) peaks 3x the daily average 17,361 /s
    Bandwidth 17,361/s × ~200 B/event — not the limit 3.47 MB/s
    HLL sketch p=14 · 16,384 regs × 6 bits — ~0.81% error 12,288 B
    Open windows 100,000 × 3 windows (1-min + 2-min grace) 300,000
    In-flight state 300,000 × 12 KB dense HLL — the ceiling 3.69 GB

    Bandwidth was never the constraint. 3.47 MB/s at peak is modest. The bottleneck is aggregation correctness — in-flight state, and how long you wait before calling a window final.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (5) Watermark grace too tight. A legitimately late click (spotty mobile network) misses its window and is excluded from the real-time number. — Fix: the reconciliation job corrects it hours later — the live dashboard is provisional, not final, until then
    • (2) Hot-key skew. One campaign_id goes viral — click volume for that key spikes 100x, overloading the partition it's pinned to. — Fix: a known limit of partitioning by campaign_id — split a hot campaign's key at ingest if it recurs
    • (7) Dedup TTL expires early. A genuinely delayed retry outlives the ~5-min window and gets double-counted. — Fix: bounded, low-probability drift, not a hard guarantee — the reconciliation job corrects it later too

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Why aggregate in the stream instead of a nightly batch report? (ref 3 — tests: near-real-time budget-pacing — a batch delay risks unrecoverable overspend)
    2. Why can't a simple incrementing counter give you "the count"? (refs 3,6 — tests: do you reach for a time axis, not just a bigger number)
    3. Why not store every click forever and count on demand? (ref 13 — tests: bounded-retention instinct, not endless rescanning)
    4. Why does the aggregator wait before emitting a window's count? (ref 5 — tests: watermark-as-deadline, not naive immediate emission)
    5. Why use a probabilistic sketch instead of a set of click IDs? (ref 6 — tests: bounded-memory instinct — 320 MB set vs. 12 KB sketch)
    6. Why does a click ping need a dedup cache at all? (ref 7 — tests: retry-awareness — the same click_id can arrive twice)

    Cover The Sheet · Answer Aloud

    Use it as revision: cover the right rail and the two panels to your left, then walk the numbered path out loud. If you cannot say why a box is there and what breaks it, that box is your gap.

    Level 3 — The Edges (Advanced)

    Pacing: 10 min ask · 25 min trace flow · 15 min read why · 40 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Ingest clicks tagged campaign_id + event-time
    • Count clicks + unique clickers, per campaign/window
    • Tolerate late, out-of-order clicks, bounded
    • Dedup retried pings — idempotent on click_id

    Non-Functional

    • Sub-minute-ish aggregate latency ← forces streaming, not batch
    • Late data can't block output forever ← forces a watermark
    • Bounded memory per campaign ← forces HLL over exact sets
    • Idempotent counting under retries ← forces click_id dedup
    • Scales as traffic grows ← forces partition-by-campaign_id

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Campaign config / budgets — elsewhere
    • Deep fraud modelling — basic filter only
    • Ad serving / creative selection

    Agree These First

    100,000 campaigns · ~5,000 avg clicks/campaign/day · 1-min windows, 2-min watermark grace. Config and fraud modelling are someone else's system. Retries aren't prevented — dedup handles them.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Click Event Source — Ad-serving client · redirect pixel
    • (2) Ingestion Log — Partitioned by campaign_id — e.g. Kafka
    • (10) Fraud + Bot Filter — Gates entry before anything is merged
    • (3) Stream Aggregator — Windowed count + HLL sketch, per campaign; campaign-partitioned; click events; peak 17,361 clicks/s
    • (4) Windowed State Store — RocksDB-backed — window counters + HLL sketches
    • (5) Watermark Tracker — emits when the watermark passes; "No more late data before T" per window
    • (7) Dedup Cache — click_id idempotency · TTL ~5 min; 300,000 entries ~ 3.69 GB
    • Late-Data Side Output — Arrived after the watermark passed
    • (8) Aggregate Store — Queryable time-series output, per campaign per window
    • (11) Checkpoint Coordinator — Exactly-once commit
    • (13) Raw Event Cold Storage — Bounded retention — e.g. 7 days
    • (12) Batch Reconciliation Job — Nightly — replays cold storage, corrects provisional counts
    • Dashboard / Advertiser Query — Reads from Aggregate Store

    Data model:

    • window_counts campaign_id+window PK · count · unique_hll · emitted_at
    • seen_click_ids click_id PK · TTL ~5 min — dedup only

    Line = flow type: COUNT · click → aggregate; QUERY · store → dashboard; CHECK · watermark / dedup; RECONCILE · corrects later.

    Say It Like This

    "This is a stream I can't fully store and can't fully wait on. I'd aggregate in the stream — windowed counts per campaign, not a nightly batch — because budget-pacing can't wait hours. A raw counter can't answer 'clicks in the last minute', so counting needs a time axis. And for unique clickers I'd reach for an HLL sketch, not a set of IDs — bounded memory, however big a campaign gets."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) Stream vs. batch Aggregate in the stream — budget-pacing can't wait hours Nightly batch → overspend nobody catches in time
    (6) Counting needs a time axis Windowed state, per campaign per minute — a real time axis One counter → a raw total, write-contended, no time axis
    (13) Bounded, not forever Cold storage keeps 7 days — enough to reconcile, not an archive Store every click forever → on-demand counting re-scans history forever
    (5) Watermark deadline An explicit deadline — "no more late data before T" Wait forever for stragglers → a window never closes
    (6) HLL vs. exact set Fixed ~12 KB sketch — memory never grows, ~1% error Exact click_id set → 320 MB for one hot campaign
    (7) Dedup cache click_id idempotency, TTL ~5 min — a retry isn't recounted No dedup → a mobile-network retry silently double-counts
    (10) Fraud filter gates entry Filters before the HLL merge — a sketch can't be un-added from Clean up after → you can't subtract a click back out
    (12) Batch reconciliation Nightly replay of bounded cold storage — corrects the miss Trust the stream forever → a missed click is gone for good

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Clicks 100,000 campaigns × 5,000 avg clicks/day 500,000,000/day
    Avg rate 500,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 5,787 /s
    Peak (×3) peaks 3x the daily average 17,361 /s
    Bandwidth 17,361/s × ~200 B/event — not the limit 3.47 MB/s
    HLL sketch p=14 · 16,384 regs × 6 bits — ~0.81% error 12,288 B
    Exact set 10M unique × 8 B hashes = 80 MB · ×4 overhead 320 MB
    HLL vs. set 80 MB ÷ 12 KB · 320 MB ÷ 12 KB 6,510x · 26,000x
    Open windows 100,000 × 3 windows (1-min + 2-min grace) 300,000
    In-flight state 300,000 × 12 KB dense HLL — the ceiling 3.69 GB
    Dedup cache 17,361/s × 300 s × 16 B (uuid) 83.3 MB

    A hot campaign is the whole argument for HLL. 320 MB in a realistic set vs. 12 KB in a sketch — 26,000x smaller, ~1% error.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (5) Watermark grace too tight. A legitimately late click (spotty mobile network) misses its window and is excluded from the real-time number. — Fix: the reconciliation job corrects it hours later — the live dashboard is provisional, not final, until then
    • (2) Hot-key skew. One campaign_id goes viral — click volume for that key spikes 100x, overloading the partition it's pinned to. — Fix: a known limit of partitioning by campaign_id — split a hot campaign's key at ingest if it recurs
    • (7) Dedup TTL expires early. A genuinely delayed retry outlives the ~5-min window and gets double-counted. — Fix: bounded, low-probability drift, not a hard guarantee — the reconciliation job corrects it later too
    • (11) Aggregator crashes mid-window. In-flight counts and HLL sketches are lost unless checkpointed. — Fix: recovery resumes from the last commit past ⑪ — never re-emits a window already sent downstream
    • (3) Consumer lag builds silently. ③ falls behind ②; the event-time vs. processing-time gap widens. — Fix: alert on lag explicitly — the dashboard looks fine and is actually minutes stale

    2. Why can't a simple incrementing counter give you "the count"? (refs 3,6 — tests: do you reach for a time axis, not just a bigger number)
    3. Why not store every click forever and count on demand? (ref 13 — tests: bounded-retention instinct, not endless rescanning)
    4. Why does the aggregator wait before emitting a window's count? (ref 5 — tests: watermark-as-deadline, not naive immediate emission)
