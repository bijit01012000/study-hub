    Prompt and context engineering

    1 sections · 427 words

    Module 05 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Treat the prompt as a versioned artefact with a contract.

    Every candidate has a prompt story, and almost all of them are the same story: some wordings were tried, one worked better, it shipped. This module tests whether the prompt is treated as a production artefact with a contract, a version and a rollback path, or as a text box nudged until the demo stopped embarrassing anyone.

    Inside this module: System prompts · Structured output · Context budget · Versioning

    Concept checkpoints

    Q5.1 — What goes in your system prompt, and what deliberately doesn't?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a rule for what belongs in the stable instruction layer versus per-request context, or just let text pile up.

    Q5.2 — You need reliable structured output from the model. How do you actually get it?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows prose instructions, function calling and constrained decoding are different mechanisms with different failure modes.

    Q5.3 — When do few shot examples earn their place, and when do they stop?

    What it really tests: Whether examples are treated as a recurring, cost bearing decision, not free accuracy added once and forgotten.

    Q5.4 — More wants to fit in your context window than will fit. How do you decide what gets in?

    What it really tests: Whether there is an explicit allocation policy under contention, or the limit gets discovered in production.

    Q5.5 — How do you ship a prompt change safely?

    What it really tests: Whether prompts are versioned, tested and revertible artefacts, or strings edited in place in a config.

    Q5.6 — The system prompt says one thing and the retrieved document says another. What happens?

    What it really tests: Whether instruction precedence is designed and enforced, especially where untrusted content shares the window.

    Q5.7 — The input does not fit in the context window. Now what?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows several strategies, their distinct failure modes, and how to pick between them.

    Q5.8 — When is fixing the prompt the wrong fix?

    What it really tests: Senior judgement, whether a prompt failure is recognised as a symptom of a retrieval, data or scoping problem.

    Q5.9 — DRILL: diagnose a failing invoice extraction prompt, specify the fix and the test that proves it.

    What it really tests: Whether a diagnosis runs before a rewording, and whether the test is defined before the change.
