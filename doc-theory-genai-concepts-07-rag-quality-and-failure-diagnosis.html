    RAG quality and failure diagnosis

    1 sections · 495 words

    Module 07 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Tell a retrieval failure from a generation failure, with evidence.

    This module tests whether a candidate ran an eval script once, or built an eval and lived with what it told them. The tell isn't whether they name a metric, it's whether they can say what ground truth that metric needed, what it missed, and what they did the week it disagreed with their intuition. Q7.8 is the module's keystone: a real, repeatable procedure for splitting a retrieval fault from a generation fault, not a general "I'd check both."

    Inside this module: Retrieval metrics · Grounding · Multi-hop · Fault isolation

    Concept checkpoints

    Q7.1 — How do you evaluate retrieval quality?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate built and maintained a real evaluation set, or names metrics and frameworks run once without knowing the ground truth.

    Q7.2 — Walk me through precision versus recall in retrieval. Which did you tune for, and why?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate understands the real trade-off, noise cost against missed-information cost, rather than reciting textbook definitions.

    Q7.3 — How do you catch a case where the model answered from its own knowledge, not the retrieved context?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a concrete detection mechanism, or relies on a prompt instruction and hopes it holds.

    Q7.4 — How do you handle grounding, citations and attribution so an answer actually traces to a real source?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows a citation isn't proof of grounding, versus treating a source link as the whole fix.

    Q7.5 — What happens when a query's answer genuinely isn't in the corpus? How do you detect that?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has an explicit detection mechanism for out-of-scope queries, or relies on the model noticing on its own.

    Q7.6 — How do you handle a question that needs information from two or more documents to answer?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows multi-hop failure is a retrieval architecture problem, not something widening k on a single search solves.

    Q7.7 — Your corpus has conflicting versions of the same document. How do you deal with that?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats this as a real production condition with a designed response, not just an ingestion hygiene problem.

    Q7.8 — You get a bad answer from the RAG pipeline. How do you tell if it's retrieval or generation at fault?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a concrete isolation procedure, the difference between "I check both" and an actual decisive method.

    Q7.9 — DRILL: Wrong answers rose this week, no deploy happened. Build your diagnostic checklist, in order.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has an internalised, ordered sequence, coverage then retrieval then generation, not an unordered bag of causes.
