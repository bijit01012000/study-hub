    Q10. Tell me about a time you delivered under a major challenge/constraint.

    4 sections · 598 words

    Core message: Under a hard deadline, I chose re-scoping visibly over performing perfection — and shipped a demo leadership could actually trust.

    Framework

    S — Situation. On Auto-RCA (LLM agent synthesizing logs, metrics, topology, thread dumps into ranked root-cause hypotheses), the leadership demo was approaching. Mid-build, evals showed ~55% accuracy on newer ticket types — fabricated-sounding hypotheses. Re-architecting in the window was impossible.

    T — Task. Deliver a demo I could defend — or refuse to show what I couldn't. I took the first.

    A — Action. Refused to demo what I couldn't defend — flaky output was a time bomb.

    • Built confidence-gated output in ~1 week: covered categories (85%+ accuracy) got full ranked hypotheses.

    • Otherwise: honest "insufficient data" state + cached historical RCA. No fabrication.

    • Cut two demo features with the PM's agreement — protect the core narrative.

    • Daily one-line stakeholder updates — nobody surprised on demo day.

    • Dry-ran with my manager against the 3 hardest questions, including "what happens when the agent doesn't know?"

    R — Result. Demo landed — 85%+ on covered categories plus honest limits read as maturity, not weakness.

    • Rollout approved with my phased gate baked into the plan.

    • The confidence gate shipped permanently — still how Auto-RCA handles low-evidence categories.

    L — Lesson. Delivering under a challenge is re-scoping visibly, not performing perfection — a demo with named limits beats one with hidden failure modes.

    Read it straight

    This was on Auto-RCA at Rakuten — the LLM agent that synthesizes logs, metrics, topology, and thread dumps into ranked root-cause hypotheses. The leadership demo was approaching, and mid-build the evals showed about 55% accuracy on newer ticket types, which meant fabricated-sounding hypotheses. Re-architecting in that window was impossible. I refused to demo what I couldn't defend — flaky output was a time bomb. In about a week, I built a graceful degradation path: confidence-gated output. Covered categories, where evals showed 85%-plus accuracy, got full ranked hypotheses; everything else showed an honest "insufficient data" state plus cached historical RCA for known patterns — honest limits, no fabrication. I cut two demo features with the PM's agreement to protect the core narrative, sent daily one-line stakeholder updates so nobody was surprised on demo day, and dry-ran with my manager against the three hardest questions leadership could ask, including "what happens when the agent doesn't know?" The demo landed. Leadership saw the 85%-plus accuracy and the honest insufficient-data state, which read as maturity, not weakness. Rollout was approved with the phased gate I proposed, and that gate ships permanently — it's still how Auto-RCA handles low-evidence categories. The lesson: delivering under a challenge means re-scoping visibly, not performing perfection. One broken demo to leadership costs months of trust; a deliberately scoped one costs nothing.

    Revision bullets

    • Auto-RCA leadership demo, mid-build evals

    • ~55% accuracy on new ticket types

    • Built confidence-gated path in ~1 week

    • Covered categories: 85%+ accuracy

    • Cut 2 features with PM's agreement

    • Demo landed; rollout approved with phased gate

    If they push deeper

    • Wouldn't a polished demo have been better? → No. One broken demo to leadership costs months of trust; a deliberately scoped one costs nothing. I've seen the inverse happen and it poisons every later claim.

    • What did you cut, and why that? → Two secondary visualization flourishes the PM agreed added demo value but no decision value. We cut by "what does leadership decide with," not by effort sunk.
