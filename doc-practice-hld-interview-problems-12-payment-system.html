    Design a Payment System

    3 sections · 3,292 words

    Three levels: Level 1 — The Shape, Level 2 — The Defence, Level 3 — The Edges.

    Level 1 — The Shape (Foundation)

    Pacing: 3 min ask · 5 min trace flow · 7 min read why.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Initiate a payment — card, UPI, or bank
    • Confirm whether a payment succeeded or failed
    • Record every attempt and outcome, immutably

    Non-Functional

    • Never double-charge one payment ← forces an idempotency key
    • Never lose a payment's true outcome ← forces reconciliation, not self-trust
    • Every change must be auditable, forever ← forces an append-only ledger

    Agree These First

    2,000,000 payment attempts/day · ~0.5% of gateway calls end unknown · ~5% are retried after an apparent failure · every payment writes 2 ledger entries. Illustrative, round numbers.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Client — Buyer — card, UPI, or bank transfer
    • (1) call
    • Payment Service — Creates a payment record + idempotency key — before calling out
    • (10) timeout? — UNKNOWN, not failure
    • Payment Gateway — Third-party processor — charges the card / UPI / bank. Outside your control.
    • Ledger — Append-only · double-entry — never updated or deleted, only appended; (12) writes 2 balanced entries, debit + credit

    Data model: payments payment_id PK → idempotency_key, status.

    Line = call vs outcome: CALL · service → gateway; CONFIRM · known outcome; UNKNOWN · timeout, no answer.

    Say It Like This

    "Money means being wrong is not an option. Every attempt gets an idempotency key, written before I ever call out — a retry can't become a double-charge. A gateway call can time out without telling me what happened — I'd never guess; I'd record it as unknown and resolve it later against the processor's own settlement record, since that's the real source of truth. And the ledger only ever appends — a refund is a new entry, not an edit, so the history stays trustworthy."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (2) Idempotency key One key per attempt — a retry is recognised as the same attempt, not a new charge No dedup → any retry of an unknown outcome that really succeeded double-charges
    (4) Append-only ledger Append-only — a refund is a new entry, never an edit to the original charge Mutable balance updates → a "fix" destroys the very history an audit needs
    (6) Reconcile, don't trust Reconcile against the processor's settlement file — their record is the truth, not yours Trust only your sync response → ~10,000/day unknown outcomes never get resolved

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Payments 2,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 23.1 /s
    Unknown/day 2,000,000 × 0.5% — the headline number 10,000 /day
    Retried/day 2,000,000 × 5% — what the key covers 100,000 /day
    Ledger writes 2,000,000 × 2 (double-entry) 4,000,000 /day
    Ledger writes 4,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 46 /s

    Round hard, out loud. ~23/s is unremarkable — the number that matters is 10,000/day: that's how often "did it work?" has no answer from your own system, every single day.

    Before You Draw Anything — The Four Moves

    1. Clarify, don't assume. Are you the merchant integrating with an external processor, or building the processor itself? Say merchant — the more common case. Multi-currency in scope? No — say so. — Two minutes of questions buys the whole design.
    2. Agree the scale out loud. "2,000,000 payment attempts a day, about 23 a second." Now every later choice has a reason. — An unstated number is an undefendable design.
    3. Draw the happy path only. Client → Payment Service → Payment Gateway → Ledger. Get agreement before adding anything. — Boxes you add before agreement are boxes you'll defend alone.
    4. Name one trade-off unprompted. "I'd never assume a timed-out payment succeeded or failed — I'd record it as unknown and resolve it by reconciling against the processor later." — This is the single move that separates L1 from a junior answer.

    What L1 Is Testing

    • Do you say "I don't know" on a timeout, not a guess?
    • Do you reach for an idempotency key unprompted?
    • Did you name append-only / auditability unprompted?

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Your payment gateway call times out. Did the charge succeed? (ref 10 — tests: do they say "I don't know" rather than guess, and know how to resolve it)
    2. A client retries a payment after a timeout. Does the customer get charged twice? (ref 2 — tests: idempotency-key awareness)
    3. Why not just store a single balance number per user instead of a full ledger? (ref 4 — tests: append-only / audit / double-entry awareness)
    4. Why write the payment record before calling the gateway, but not the ledger entries? (refs 1,4 — tests: a record to reconcile against, but you post to the ledger only once money moved)
    5. Card, UPI, or bank transfer — does the design change per payment method? (ref 3 — tests: can they name the gateway as the abstraction that absorbs that difference)

    At L1 you are being judged on one thing: can you produce a coherent shape and defend three choices inside it. Depth is page 2 — do not reach for it here. Ghosted boxes are what arrives next; you should be able to guess two of them.

    Level 2 — The Defence (Intermediate)

    Pacing: 5 min ask · 15 min trace flow · 10 min read why · 30 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Initiate a payment — card, UPI, or bank
    • Confirm whether a payment succeeded or failed
    • Record every attempt and outcome, immutably
    • Handle refunds
    • Reconcile against the processor's settlement report

    Non-Functional

    • Never double-charge one payment ← forces an idempotency key
    • Never lose a payment's true outcome ← forces reconciliation, not self-trust
    • Every change must be auditable, forever ← forces an append-only ledger
    • A gateway call can time out silently ← forces "unknown" as its own state

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Currency conversion / multi-currency
    • Building the processor itself — you integrate with a gateway
    • Fraud / risk scoring models

    Agree These First

    2,000,000 payment attempts/day · ~0.5% of gateway calls end unknown · ~5% are retried after an apparent failure · every payment writes 2 ledger entries. Illustrative, round numbers.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Client — Buyer — card, UPI, or bank transfer
    • (1) call
    • (11) Idempotency Store — Checked FIRST — have I already attempted this exact payment?
    • Payment Service — Creates a payment record + idempotency key — before calling out
    • (10) timeout? — UNKNOWN, not failure
    • Payment Gateway — Third-party processor — charges the card / UPI / bank. Outside your control.
    • (5) Webhook Receiver — A second, independent channel — confirms status even if the call timed out
    • Reconciliation Job — Periodic — pulls the processor's settlement file, resolves every unknown; resolves ~10,000/day unknowns
    • Ledger — Append-only · double-entry — never updated or deleted, only appended; (12) writes 2 balanced entries, debit + credit; 4,000,000 writes/day ≈ 46/s

    Data model:

    • payments payment_id PK · idempotency_key · amount · status · created_at
    • ledger_entries entry_id PK · payment_id · account · debit/credit · amount — append-only

    Line = call vs outcome: CALL · service → gateway; CONFIRM · known outcome; UNKNOWN · timeout, no answer.

    Say It Like This

    "Money means being wrong is not an option. Every attempt gets an idempotency key, written before I ever call out — a retry can't become a double-charge. A gateway call can time out without telling me what happened — I'd never guess; I'd record it as unknown and resolve it later against the processor's own settlement record, since that's the real source of truth. And the ledger only ever appends — a refund is a new entry, not an edit, so the history stays trustworthy."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (2) Idempotency key One key per attempt — a retry is recognised as the same attempt, not a new charge No dedup → any retry of an unknown outcome that really succeeded double-charges
    (4) Append-only ledger Append-only — a refund is a new entry, never an edit to the original charge Mutable balance updates → a "fix" destroys the very history an audit needs
    (6) Reconcile, don't trust Reconcile against the processor's settlement file — their record is the truth, not yours Trust only your sync response → ~10,000/day unknown outcomes never get resolved
    (5) Async webhook confirmation A second, independent channel — confirms status even when the sync call timed out Rely only on the sync response → a dropped connection leaves you with nothing

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Payments 2,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 23.1 /s
    Unknown/day 2,000,000 × 0.5% — the headline number 10,000 /day
    Retried/day 2,000,000 × 5% — what the key covers 100,000 /day
    Ledger writes 2,000,000 × 2 (double-entry) 4,000,000 /day
    Ledger writes 4,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 46 /s

    100,000/day retried requests is what idempotency covers. Most of those retry a clean failure and are harmless. The dangerous ones are the retries that land on an unknown outcome that actually succeeded — you cannot tell the two apart, so you key every attempt.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (10) Timeout treated as failure. A naive retry-on-timeout double-charges the customer when the original charge actually succeeded. — Fix: the idempotency key ensures a retry with the same key is recognised as the same attempt
    • (1) Timeout treated as success. The customer isn't charged, but the system believes they are — goods or services get given away for money never received. — Fix: never assume success on ambiguity — treat it as unknown, resolve via reconciliation
    • (4) A ledger entry gets silently corrected. Editing or deleting a wrong balance destroys the very auditability the ledger exists for. — Fix: append-only by design — a correction is always a new entry, never a mutation

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Your payment gateway call times out. Did the charge succeed? (ref 10 — tests: do they say "I don't know" rather than guess, and know how to resolve it)
    2. A client retries a payment after a timeout. Does the customer get charged twice? (ref 2 — tests: idempotency-key awareness)
    3. Why not just store a single balance number per user instead of a full ledger? (ref 4 — tests: append-only / audit / double-entry awareness)
    4. Why write the payment record before calling the gateway, but not the ledger entries? (refs 1,4 — tests: a record to reconcile against, but you post to the ledger only once money moved)
    5. Card, UPI, or bank transfer — does the design change per payment method? (ref 3 — tests: can they name the gateway as the abstraction that absorbs that difference)
    6. How do you know your internal records actually match what the processor really processed? (ref 6 — tests: reconciliation awareness — the processor as external source of truth)
    7. A refund request gets sent twice because of a client retry. What happens? (ref 7 — tests: idempotency applies beyond just the initial charge)

    Cover The Sheet · Answer Aloud

    Use it as revision: cover the right rail and the two panels to your left, then walk the numbered path out loud. If you cannot say why a box is there and what breaks it, that box is your gap.

    Level 3 — The Edges (Advanced)

    Pacing: 10 min ask · 25 min trace flow · 15 min read why · 40 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Initiate a payment — card, UPI, or bank
    • Confirm whether a payment succeeded or failed
    • Record every attempt and outcome, immutably
    • Handle refunds
    • Reconcile against the processor's settlement report

    Non-Functional

    • Never double-charge one payment ← forces an idempotency key
    • Never lose a payment's true outcome ← forces reconciliation, not self-trust
    • Every change must be auditable, forever ← forces an append-only ledger
    • A gateway call can time out silently ← forces "unknown" as its own state
    • Totals must stay internally consistent ← forces double-entry bookkeeping

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Currency conversion / multi-currency
    • Building the processor itself — you integrate with a gateway
    • Fraud / risk scoring models

    Agree These First

    2,000,000 payment attempts/day · ~0.5% of gateway calls end unknown · ~5% are retried after an apparent failure · every payment writes 2 ledger entries. Illustrative, round numbers.

    Components and flow:

    • Client — Buyer — card, UPI, or bank transfer

    • Ledger — Append-only · double-entry — never updated or deleted, only appended; (12) writes 2 balanced entries, debit + credit; 4,000,000 writes/day ≈ 46/s
    • (7) Refund — A new compensating entry — never edits the original charge
    • (8) Balance Integrity Check — Internal only — every debit has a matching credit, sums to zero
    • (9) Dispute / Chargeback — Reconstructed from the immutable ledger, months later — out of deep scope

    Data model:

    • payments payment_id PK · idempotency_key · amount · status · created_at
    • ledger_entries entry_id PK · payment_id · account · debit/credit · amount — append-only

    Decision Prefer Avoid

    (5) Async webhook confirmation A second, independent channel — confirms status even when the sync call timed out Rely only on the sync response → a dropped connection leaves you with nothing
    (12) Double-entry bookkeeping Every transaction touches two balanced accounts — an error becomes mathematically detectable Single-number balance updates → an error is silently wrong, never caught

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Payments 2,000,000 ÷ 86,400 s 23.1 /s
    Unknown/day 2,000,000 × 0.5% — the headline number 10,000 /day
    Retried/day 2,000,000 × 5% — what the key covers 100,000 /day

    4,000,000 ledger writes/day (46/s) is the cost of double-entry — every payment, known or not, appends exactly two balanced rows. That is what buys a books-must-balance guarantee instead of a silently-wrong number.

    • (6) Reconciliation falls behind or fails silently. Unresolved unknowns pile up unnoticed because the payment service itself still looks healthy from the outside. — Fix: monitor reconciliation lag explicitly — "alert on lag, not error rate," the same lesson as elsewhere in this set
    • (7) A refund gets processed twice. Without idempotency on refunds specifically, a retried refund request double-refunds the customer. — Fix: the same idempotency-key discipline applies to every money-moving operation, not only the charge
