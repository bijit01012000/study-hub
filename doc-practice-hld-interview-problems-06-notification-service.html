    Design a Notification Service

    3 sections · 3,028 words

    Three levels: Level 1 — The Shape, Level 2 — The Defence, Level 3 — The Edges.

    Level 1 — The Shape (Foundation)

    Pacing: 3 min ask · 5 min trace flow · 7 min read why.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Send a notification (push, SMS, email) on an internal event
    • Retry on transient failure: timeout, rate limit, 5xx
    • Never duplicate a send for the same event, even retried

    Non-Functional

    • Won't silently vanish on failure ← forces retry + backoff
    • Never duplicates a send ← forces an idempotency key
    • Failures stay visible after max retries ← forces a DLQ, not a discard

    Agree These First

    20,000,000 notifications/day · ~2% need a retry · ~0.2% exhaust retries into the DLQ. Illustrative, rounded. Every number here falls out of these three.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Event Producer — e.g. Order Service — another internal service, not a user
    • Notification Service — Decides channel + content · calls the provider · owns the retry
    • event — ≈231/s
    • (2) Provider — The provider, for now — push, SMS, email all resolve here
    • Push Provider

    Data model: notifications notification_id PK → event_id, channel, status.

    Line = call vs confirmation: DELIVERED · provider ack; CALL · service → provider.

    Outbound = untrusted here, not inbound.

    Say It Like This

    "Three ways this goes wrong: the provider fails, the same event fires twice, or a bad token fails forever. So I'd retry with backoff, not immediately — hammering a struggling provider makes it worse. I'd check an idempotency key, event ID plus channel, before every send, so a retry can't become a duplicate. And I'd give up after a bounded number of attempts into a dead-letter queue, rather than retrying forever and hiding a failure that needs a human."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (10) Retry with backoff Exponential backoff — gives a struggling provider room to recover Immediate retry → piles onto a provider that is already failing
    (11) Idempotency key Dedup key = (event_id, channel), checked before send No dedup → a retry or a duplicate event sends it twice
    (12) Dead-letter queue Bounded retries, then DLQ — failure becomes finite and visible Retry forever → wastes resources, hides that a human needs to look

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Notifications 20 M/day ÷ 86 400 s 231 /s
    Retry volume 20 M × 2% = 400 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 4.6 /s
    DLQ volume 20 M × 0.2% = 40 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 0.46 /s

    Round hard, out loud. About 230 sends a second — the exact number matters less than reaching for it unprompted. What's actually new here: unlike every earlier sheet, the thing that can fail is outside your servers.

    Before You Draw Anything — The Four Moves

    1. Clarify, don't assume. Which channels are in scope day one — push only, or push + SMS + email? Is per-user channel preference in scope? — Two minutes of questions buys the whole design.
    2. Agree the scale out loud. "Let's say 20 M notifications a day, ~231 a second." Now every later choice has a reason. — An unstated number is an undefendable design.
    3. Draw the happy path only. Producer → service → provider. Get agreement before adding anything. — Boxes you add before agreement are boxes you'll defend alone.
    4. Name one trade-off unprompted. "I'd retry with backoff, not immediately, because hammering a struggling provider makes it worse." — This is the single move that separates L1 from a junior answer.

    What L1 Is Testing

    • Can you produce a shape without being led to it?
    • Does every box have a reason you volunteered?
    • Did you say "retry ≠ immediately" before being asked?

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. A provider times out. Do you retry immediately? (ref 10 — tests: backoff awareness, avoiding pile-on)
    2. The same event gets published twice by the producer. Does the user get two notifications? (ref 11 — tests: idempotency-key awareness)
    3. A user's push token is permanently invalid. What happens after retry 1, 2, 3... forever? (refs 9,12 — tests: dead-letter / bounded-retry awareness)

    At L1 you are being judged on one thing: can you produce a coherent shape and defend three choices inside it. Depth is page 2 — do not reach for it here. Ghosted boxes are what arrives next; you should be able to guess two of them.

    Level 2 — The Defence (Intermediate)

    Pacing: 5 min ask · 15 min trace flow · 10 min read why · 30 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Send a notification (push, SMS, email) on an internal event
    • Retry on transient failure: timeout, rate limit, 5xx
    • Never duplicate a send for the same event, even retried
    • Route to channel/provider by type + preference
    • Give up after N failures — land in a DLQ

    Non-Functional

    • Won't silently vanish on failure ← forces retry + backoff
    • Never duplicates a send ← forces an idempotency key
    • Failures stay visible after max retries ← forces a DLQ, not a discard
    • Providers rate-limit, go down ← forces backoff + circuit-breaking
    • OTP is urgent, a digest isn't ← forces priority-aware queuing

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Composing / templating content
    • In-app notification centre, unread badges
    • Preference UI — assume the table exists

    Agree These First

    20,000,000 notifications/day · ~2% need a retry · ~0.2% exhaust retries into the DLQ. Illustrative, rounded. Every number here falls out of these three.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Event Producer — e.g. Order Service — another internal service, not a user
    • (4) Notification Service — Decides channel + content · calls the provider · owns the retry
    • event — ≈231/s
    • (3) Idempotency Store — Dedup key = event_id + channel — checked before send; 48 h rolling window ≈ 2 GB
    • Push Provider — APNs / FCM — push, external, outside your control
    • SMS Provider — Twilio-class gateway — its own rate limit, its own outages
    • Email Provider — ESP — different failure shape again: bounces, soft-fails
    • (7) Retry Queue — Failed sends land here — backoff before re-attempt; ≈4.6/s need at least one retry
    • (8) Delivery Log — Durable record of every attempt + outcome, for audit
    • (13) Priority-aware queuing — OTP-class fast lane, bulk-class slow lane
    • (14) Per-provider circuit breaker — fail fast once error rate crosses a threshold

    Data model:

    • notifications notification_id PK · event_id · channel · idempotency_key · status · attempts
    • providers channel PK → adapter, rate_limit, circuit_state

    Line = call vs confirmation: DELIVERED · provider ack; CALL · service → provider; RETRY · off critical path.

    Say It Like This

    "Three ways this goes wrong: the provider fails, the same event fires twice, or a bad token fails forever. So I'd retry with backoff, not immediately — hammering a struggling provider makes it worse. I'd check an idempotency key, event ID plus channel, before every send, so a retry can't become a duplicate. And I'd give up after a bounded number of attempts into a dead-letter queue, rather than retrying forever and hiding a failure that needs a human."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (10) Retry with backoff Exponential backoff — gives a struggling provider room to recover Immediate retry → piles onto a provider that is already failing
    (11) Idempotency key Dedup key = (event_id, channel), checked before send No dedup → a retry or a duplicate event sends it twice
    (12) Dead-letter queue Bounded retries, then DLQ — failure becomes finite and visible Retry forever → wastes resources, hides that a human needs to look
    (13) Priority-aware queuing Fast lane for OTP-class, slow lane for bulk/digest One FIFO → a bulk digest queued ahead of an OTP delays it for nothing
    (14) Per-provider circuit breaker Trip per provider past a failure threshold — fail fast into retry-later Keep hammering a down provider → every send waits out a timeout it was always going to lose

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Notifications 20 M/day ÷ 86 400 s 231 /s
    Retry volume 20 M × 2% = 400 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 4.6 /s
    DLQ volume 20 M × 0.2% = 40 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 0.46 /s
    Dedup keys 20 M/day × 48 h retention window 40,000,000
    Key store 40 M keys × ~50 B (event_id + channel + overhead) ≈2 GB

    ≈0.46/s into the dead-letter queue — about one every two seconds. Small enough for a human to actually review. That is the point of a DLQ: a safety net, not a queue you process at scale.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (14) Provider outage. A whole channel goes down; without a circuit breaker every send times out slowly instead of failing fast. — Fix: circuit breaker per provider — fail fast into retry-later past a failure threshold
    • (10) Retry storm. Many failed sends retry at the same backoff interval, hitting the provider in synchronized waves right as it recovers. — Fix: jittered backoff — same fix family as sheet 03's thundering reconnect
    • (11) Duplicate notification. A retry, or a duplicate event from the producer, sends the same notification twice. — Fix: idempotency key checked before send

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. A provider times out. Do you retry immediately? (ref 10 — tests: backoff awareness, avoiding pile-on)
    2. The same event gets published twice by the producer. Does the user get two notifications? (ref 11 — tests: idempotency-key awareness)
    3. A user's push token is permanently invalid. What happens after retry 1, 2, 3... forever? (refs 9,12 — tests: dead-letter / bounded-retry awareness)
    4. An OTP and a weekly digest are queued at the same moment. Which arrives first, and does that matter? (ref 13 — tests: priority-queue awareness)
    5. Your push provider is down for ten minutes. What does your system do differently in minute 1 versus minute 8? (ref 14 — tests: circuit-breaker awareness, not a fixed dumb loop)

    Cover The Sheet · Answer Aloud

    Use it as revision: cover the right rail and the two panels to your left, then walk the numbered path out loud. If you cannot say why a box is there and what breaks it, that box is your gap.

    Level 3 — The Edges (Advanced)

    Pacing: 10 min ask · 25 min trace flow · 15 min read why · 40 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Send a notification (push, SMS, email) on an internal event
    • Retry on transient failure: timeout, rate limit, 5xx
    • Never duplicate a send for the same event, even retried
    • Route to channel/provider by type + preference
    • Give up after N failures — land in a DLQ

    Non-Functional

    • Won't silently vanish on failure ← forces retry + backoff
    • Never duplicates a send ← forces an idempotency key
    • Failures stay visible after max retries ← forces a DLQ, not a discard
    • Providers rate-limit, go down ← forces backoff + circuit-breaking
    • OTP is urgent, a digest isn't ← forces priority-aware queuing

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Composing / templating content
    • In-app notification centre, unread badges
    • Preference UI — assume the table exists

    Agree These First

    20,000,000 notifications/day · ~2% need a retry · ~0.2% exhaust retries into the DLQ. Illustrative, rounded. Every number here falls out of these three.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Event Producer — e.g. Order Service — another internal service, not a user

    • event — ≈231/s

    • (13) Priority-aware queuing — OTP-class fast lane, bulk-class slow lane
    • (14) Per-provider circuit breaker — fail fast once error rate crosses a threshold
    • (15) Provider adapters — a thin adapter per channel
    • Dead Letter Queue — Terminal after N attempts — a human reviews, not a script; ≈0.46/s · ~1 every 2 s

    Data model:

    • notifications notification_id PK · event_id · channel · idempotency_key · status · attempts
    • providers channel PK → adapter, rate_limit, circuit_state

    Line = call vs confirmation: DELIVERED · provider ack; CALL · service → provider; RETRY · off critical path.

    Decision Prefer Avoid

    (11) Idempotency key Dedup key = (event_id, channel), checked before send No dedup → a retry or a duplicate event sends it twice
    (12) Dead-letter queue Bounded retries, then DLQ — failure becomes finite and visible Retry forever → wastes resources, hides that a human needs to look
    (13) Priority-aware queuing Fast lane for OTP-class, slow lane for bulk/digest One FIFO → a bulk digest queued ahead of an OTP delays it for nothing
    (14) Per-provider circuit breaker Trip per provider past a failure threshold — fail fast into retry-later Keep hammering a down provider → every send waits out a timeout it was always going to lose
    (15) Provider adapters A thin adapter per channel — isolates each API's quirks One generic send() → provider quirks leak into the core service

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Notifications 20 M/day ÷ 86 400 s 231 /s
    Retry volume 20 M × 2% = 400 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 4.6 /s
    DLQ volume 20 M × 0.2% = 40 K/day ÷ 86 400 s 0.46 /s
    Dedup keys 20 M/day × 48 h retention window 40,000,000
    Key store 40 M keys × ~50 B (event_id + channel + overhead) ≈2 GB

    The idempotency store is bounded, not open-ended. ≈2 GB on a rolling 48-hour window — old keys expire, so this stays flat forever. Contrast sheet 03's Message Store, which has no ceiling at all.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (14) Provider outage. A whole channel goes down; without a circuit breaker every send times out slowly instead of failing fast. — Fix: circuit breaker per provider — fail fast into retry-later past a failure threshold
    • (10) Retry storm. Many failed sends retry at the same backoff interval, hitting the provider in synchronized waves right as it recovers. — Fix: jittered backoff — same fix family as sheet 03's thundering reconnect
    • (11) Duplicate notification. A retry, or a duplicate event from the producer, sends the same notification twice. — Fix: idempotency key checked before send
    • (9) Poison message. A bad token or invalid number retries forever with no DLQ. — Fix: bounded retry count, then dead-letter
    • (13) Priority inversion. A burst of bulk sends delays a time-sensitive OTP stuck behind them in one FIFO queue. — Fix: priority-aware queuing

    1. A provider times out. Do you retry immediately? (ref 10 — tests: backoff awareness, avoiding pile-on)
    2. The same event gets published twice by the producer. Does the user get two notifications? (ref 11 — tests: idempotency-key awareness)

    5. Your push provider is down for ten minutes. What does your system do differently in minute 1 versus minute 8? (ref 14 — tests: circuit-breaker awareness, not a fixed dumb loop)
    6. How do you avoid a thundering herd against a provider the moment it comes back up after an outage? (ref 10 — tests: jittered backoff, ties to the retry-storm failure)
