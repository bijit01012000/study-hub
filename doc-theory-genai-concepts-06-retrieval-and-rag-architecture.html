    Retrieval and RAG architecture

    1 sections · 761 words

    Module 06 · 15 questions · 1 practical drill

    Design retrieval deliberately instead of wiring a tutorial.

    This module is the depth test for RAG. Every candidate can draw a vector database box and an LLM box with an arrow between them. The real signal is what chunk size they used and why, what happens when the embedding model goes stale, and what trade-off they made picking a vector store. That is where an assembled tutorial system falls apart and a designed one holds up. Questions Q6.10 to Q6.15 cover ingestion, query rewriting, contextual retrieval, permissions and embedding migration, and live in a companion file.

    Inside this module: Parsing & ingestion · Chunking · Query rewriting · Permissions

    Concept checkpoints

    Q6.1 — Draw a production RAG pipeline end to end, from a document landing in storage to the final answer.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate designed a full pipeline with real decision points, or only knows the embed, search, prompt-stuffing loop from a tutorial.

    Q6.2 — What chunking strategy did you use, and how did you actually land on that size?

    What it really tests: Whether chunk size came from measuring retrieval quality against real document structure, or was copied from a tutorial default.

    Q6.3 — What actually breaks when a document gets split at a chunk boundary?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has diagnosed a real boundary failure, or only knows overlap is the textbook answer without knowing its limits.

    Q6.4 — How did you choose your embedding model, and what would changing it later actually cost?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows embedding spaces are model-specific with a real cost to change, or treats the model as a swappable config value.

    Q6.5 — How did you choose your vector database, and what trade-off did you actually make?

    What it really tests: Whether a real constraint, corpus size, existing infrastructure, ops capacity, drove the choice, or the candidate picked the marketed vendor.

    Q6.6 — How do you combine metadata filtering with vector search, and where does hybrid search fit in?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate understands pre-filter versus post-filter mechanics, and why dense-only retrieval misses exact-match terms.

    Q6.7 — When does adding a reranker actually earn its latency in a RAG pipeline?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate diagnoses a precision problem before reaching for a reranker, or adds one because it's considered best practice.

    Q6.8 — How do you keep your index fresh as the underlying documents keep changing?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a real incremental-update strategy with a cost model, or reindexes everything nightly and calls that freshness.

    Q6.9 — DRILL: 50,000 documents, a 2-second retrieval-and-generation budget. Design the retrieval side.

    What it really tests: Forces sizing the problem in real vectors and allocating a latency budget stage by stage, rather than reaching for every technique at once.

    Q6.10 — How does your ingestion pipeline turn a real PDF into clean text without losing tables or layout?

    What it really tests: Whether parsing was engineered against messy real documents, or the candidate assumes a PDF library's raw text extraction is good enough for retrieval.

    Q6.11 — Walk me through what happens to a user's raw query before it ever reaches your retriever.

    What it really tests: Whether query rewriting is a real, tested stage, or the raw user query is assumed to already be the best possible search query.

    Q6.12 — What do you add to a chunk before embedding it, beyond the raw text itself?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate enriches chunks with context that survives the split, or embeds raw fragments and hopes they stand alone.

    Q6.13 — How do you stop retrieval from ever returning a chunk the current user isn't allowed to see?

    What it really tests: Whether permissions are enforced server-side at query time, or bolted on by filtering results after the fact.

    Q6.14 — A user's access to a document gets revoked. How long before your index actually stops returning it to them?

    What it really tests: Whether revocation propagates into the index with a known bound, or lags behind the source of truth indefinitely.

    Q6.15 — How do you move the whole corpus to a new embedding model without taking retrieval offline?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate designs a reversible migration, or plans to swap models in place and hope nothing breaks.
