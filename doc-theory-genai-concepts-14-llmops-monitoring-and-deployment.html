    LLMOps, monitoring and deployment

    1 sections · 519 words

    Module 14 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Operate a service with no simple correctness signal.

    This is the module where the CV line stops being a demo and becomes an operational commitment. A trace with no document ids, a prompt that ships outside code review, a provider that can change a model under you with no deploy on your side, none of that is covered by saying "we had logs and a dashboard." The follow-ups probe whether a candidate has actually been paged for this system, or has only watched it work once in a demo.

    Inside this module: Tracing · Drift without labels · Safe rollout · Provider failure

    Concept checkpoints

    Q14.1 — Walk me through tracing one request through retrieval, rerank, generation and a tool call, end to end.

    What it really tests: Whether tracing is built around the pipeline's real stages with enough state to explain a decision later, or is just request-level logging.

    Q14.2 — Your feature has no pass or fail test suite. What do you actually monitor once it is live?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has cheap, always-on proxy signals for a system with no ground truth, separate from the offline eval Module 8 covers.

    Q14.3 — Your model provider deprecates the version you run in production. What do you actually do?

    What it really tests: Whether version pinning and provider risk are owned with a concrete response plan, not treated as the vendor's problem alone.

    Q14.4 — You believe a new prompt or model version is better, not forced. How do you get it into production safely?

    What it really tests: Whether a change is treated as a staged rollout with traffic splitting and a rollback path, or a config edit pushed to everyone at once.

    Q14.5 — Nobody's labelled anything, no user complained, but answers seem quietly worse. How do you catch that?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can name concrete drift signals that need no ground-truth labels, distinct from building a graded eval set.

    Q14.6 — A prompt shipped through config this morning, not a deploy, and things got worse. Roll it back?

    What it really tests: Whether prompt-as-config changes have their own rollback tooling, since the usual code-deploy safety net doesn't cover them.

    Q14.7 — Your provider is rate-limiting you, or is down. What happens to your feature, and what should happen?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has designed for availability under provider failure, backoff, queueing, degradation, not just assumed uptime.

    Q14.8 — How would you compare two model versions before committing, without exposing every user to the worse one?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can design shadow deployment or a genuine A/B split with a defined metric and stopping rule, not a vibe check.

    Q14.9 — DRILL: overnight the assistant got hedgier with fewer citations. Nobody deployed. Write the runbook.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate assembles tracing, drift detection and rollback into one ordered incident procedure, not isolated concepts.
