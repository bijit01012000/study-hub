    Making the switch credible

    1 sections · 529 words

    Module 01 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Explain the move without overclaiming or apologising for it.

    This module is not about proving you can define RAG or explain an agent loop, other modules cover that. It is about whether you can talk about a career move honestly: without pretending you have shipped something you have not, without turning a weakness into a secret strength, and without answering a framework question by bluffing or apologising. The reader has not shipped GenAI in production, and every question here assumes that. The weak answers are not ignorant, they are fluent, evasive, over-rehearsed or quietly self-flattering, and the follow-ups exist to find the one real instance behind the general claim.

    Inside this module: Why now · Mapping experience · Honest gaps · The narrative

    How to work this module: Answer each question out loud in 90 seconds before reading the model answer. Then check you named a number, the alternative you rejected, and what actually failed. Those three are what the follow-ups are hunting for.

    Concept checkpoints

    Q1.1 — Most of your experience is backend or full-stack. Why GenAI now?

    What it really tests: Whether the move is grounded in something the candidate actually did, or is a restatement of market hype with the candidate absent from their own story.

    Q1.2 — What in your current work is actually relevant to a GenAI role?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate maps specific skills onto the new role, rather than listing generic strengths that could apply to any job.

    Q1.3 — You have not shipped this in production. Why should we hire you?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can make an honest, evidenced case for themselves without overclaiming or collapsing into apology.

    Q1.4 — Walk me through the GenAI side project on your CV.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can describe a proof of concept accurately, without letting it slide into sounding like production.

    Q1.5 — In your own words, what counts as real GenAI experience?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a clear, honest line between experience and exposure, rather than inflating both to look bigger.

    Q1.6 — Your CV says "worked with LLMs". What does that actually mean?

    What it really tests: Whether a vague CV phrase resolves into something specific and checkable, or evaporates under one direct question.

    Q1.7 — Where do you expect to struggle in your first 90 days?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate names a real, specific weakness or dresses up a strength as one to sound safe.

    Q1.8 — Why did you build directly against model APIs instead of using a framework?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate maps their own work onto what the framework does, rather than bluffing familiarity or apologising for not using it.

    Q1.9 — [DRILL] Build a 90 second switch narrative that survives three follow-up questions.

    What it really tests: Whether the story from earlier questions compresses into something specific enough to hold up under pressure, not a longer story that only works uninterrupted.
