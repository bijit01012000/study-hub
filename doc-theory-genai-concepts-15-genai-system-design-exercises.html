    GenAI system-design exercises

    1 sections · 599 words

    Module 15 · 10 questions · 1 practical drill

    Drive the blank page when the expensive component is non-deterministic.

    Nobody asks you to design Dropbox any more. What separates candidates here is that the expensive component isn't a database that returns the same answer every time, it's a model that costs money per token, takes a variable amount of time to answer, and might be confidently wrong in a way no assertion catches. Every question below carries a number, a corpus size, a latency budget, a cost ceiling, a scale figure, because a design question without a constraint is a conversation, and this round is not a conversation.

    Inside this module: Requirements · Capacity estimates · Architecture · Trade-offs

    How to work this module: alternative you rejected, and what actually failed. Those three are what the follow-ups are hunting for.

    Concept checkpoints

    Q15.1 — Design a legal-team Q&A system: 2 million documents, under 3-second answers, with checkable citations.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats retrieval as one part of a latency budget, not the whole task, and builds citations as a structural mechanism, not a prompt ask.

    Q15.2 — Design a support assistant for 40,000 tickets a month that can never promise an unauthorised refund.

    What it really tests: Whether escalation and refund authority are enforced by a deterministic gate outside the model, not trusted to a prompt and self-reported confidence.

    Q15.3 — Design a code-review bot for a 12-million-line monorepo: comments within 5 minutes, low false positives.

    What it really tests: Whether review cost and latency scale with the diff rather than the repo, and whether false positives are treated as a compounding trust cost.

    Q15.4 — Add semantic search beside a keyword search that hits 150ms p99, without regressing its working 90%.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats this as an integration and rollout problem against a working system, rather than a green-field retrieval build.

    Q15.5 — Design a voice support agent handling 30,000 daily calls: first audio under 800ms, even with a KB lookup.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate fits knowledge-base retrieval inside the voice latency budget as a first-class constraint, and scales the design, not just the turn pipeline.

    Q15.6 — Design an ops agent that can restart services and open tickets without one bad call taking down more.

    What it really tests: Whether permissioning and blast-radius containment are enforced at the tool-execution layer, not trusted to the model's planning or a prompt instruction.

    Q15.7 — Design a description pipeline generating 5,000 pieces weekly, all reviewed by a 4-person team.

    What it really tests: Whether human review is recognised as the real throughput ceiling, a human-hours problem, rather than a formality layered on top of generation speed.

    Q15.8 — Design an eval and monitoring platform for 500,000 daily model calls across four AI features.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate designs shared observability infrastructure, storage tiers and sampling, rather than describing which metrics matter without building it.

    Q15.9 — A meeting-summary feature hits 200,000 MAU. Produce the cost and peak-capacity numbers before building.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can chain a usage assumption through calls, tokens and cost to a number, separating average load from the peak it's provisioned for.

    Q15.10 — DRILL: design a ticket-search tool for 1.2 million tickets in 20 minutes, 5-second budget, cited answers.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate independently assembles constraint-first framing, a decomposed budget and verified citations, without being walked through it.
