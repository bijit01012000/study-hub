    Design a Trading System

    3 sections · 3,365 words

    Three levels: Level 1 — The Shape, Level 2 — The Defence, Level 3 — The Edges.

    Level 1 — The Shape (Foundation)

    Pacing: 3 min ask · 5 min trace flow · 7 min read why.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Accept orders — place, cancel, modify
    • Assign each event a strict, monotonic sequence number

    Non-Functional

    • Tick-to-trade in low hundreds of us ← forces a single writer
    • Deterministic, auditable ordering ← forces sequence numbers

    Agree These First

    1 us to sequence, a 500 us total tickto-trade budget, a 1,000,000-event snapshot interval. Round, illustrative numbers, not any named exchange's.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Trading Client — Places, cancels, modifies — one of thousands, concurrently
    • (2) Gateway — Validates orders · forwards to the Sequencer — never assigns order itself
    • (3) Sequencer — The single writer — assigns a strict, monotonic sequence number to every event
    • (4) Event Log — Durable, append-only, strictly ordered — the sole source of truth
    • orders — avg 200,000/s · peak 600,000/s

    Data model: events seq_no PK → type, payload.

    Line = flow type: WRITE · order → log; CONFIRM · exec report.

    Say It Like This

    "This needs total order inside a budget that can't afford agreement between writers, so there's exactly one Sequencer — order is decided by arrival, not consensus. It hands out a strict, monotonic sequence number, and that number — never wall-clock time — is the one true ordering authority. Everything downstream is a pure function of replaying that log, which is why the log, not the book, is the source of truth."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) One writer, not consensus Order decided by arrival at one place — nothing to agree on A vote/consensus round costs far more than the latency budget allows
    (2) Gateway forwards, never assigns Only the Sequencer ever assigns order — one authority, one total order Every gateway assigning order → no single total order exists anywhere
    (4) The log is the truth, not the book Durable and replayable — everything else is rebuilt FROM it In-memory state is lost on crash, or drifts silently between replicas

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Budget tick-to-trade, Gateway to published report 500 us
    Ceiling 1 us to sequence + append, single-threaded 1,000,000 /s
    Avg load place + cancel + modify, baseline 200,000 /s
    Peak (×3) 3x average — tick-to-trade must still hold 600,000 /s
    Utilization 600,000 ÷ 1,000,000 ceiling — comfortable headroom 60%

    Round hard, out loud. The Sequencer's own step is the FASTEST stage in the whole pipeline — 1 us out of a 500 us budget — deliberately, so it is never the bottleneck. Coordination is what would be slow; a single writer has none.

    Before You Draw Anything — The Four Moves

    1. Clarify, don't assume. What's the tick-to-trade budget? Is exactly-once matching in scope, or just ordering and durability? — Two minutes of questions buys the whole design.
    2. Agree the scale out loud. "200,000 events/s average, 600,000 at peak, a 500 us tick-to-trade budget." Now every choice has a reason. — An unstated number is an undefendable design.
    3. Draw the happy path only. Client → Gateway → Sequencer → Event Log. Get agreement before adding anything. — Boxes you add before agreement are boxes you'll defend alone.
    4. Name one trade-off unprompted. "The sequence number, never wall-clock time, is the ordering authority — clocks skew and jump, counters don't." — This is the single move that separates L1 from a junior answer.

    What L1 Is Testing

    • Do you name a single-writer sequencer, not a distributed lock?
    • Do you reach for a sequence number over a timestamp, and say why?
    • Did you ask about the latency budget before drawing anything?

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Why not let a few gateways accept orders and agree on order afterward? (ref 3 — tests: any inter-writer coordination costs more than the budget allows)
    2. Why does the Gateway forward orders to the Sequencer instead of stamping order itself? (refs 2,3 — tests: multiple stampers means no single total order exists)
    3. The in-memory book already looks correct. Why is the Event Log still "the" truth? (ref 4 — tests: durability over a state that can silently diverge)

    At L1 you are being judged on one thing: can you produce a coherent shape and defend three choices inside it. Depth is page 2 — do not reach for it here. Ghosted boxes are what arrives next; you should be able to guess two of them.

    Level 2 — The Defence (Intermediate)

    Pacing: 5 min ask · 15 min trace flow · 10 min read why · 30 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Accept orders — place, cancel, modify
    • Assign each event a strict, monotonic sequence number
    • Match orders, price-time priority, from the sequenced log
    • Publish execution reports to subscribers

    Non-Functional

    • Tick-to-trade in low hundreds of us ← forces a single writer
    • Deterministic, auditable ordering ← forces sequence numbers
    • The book must never drift on its own ← forces event sourcing
    • Crash recovery can't replay from #1 ← forces periodic snapshots

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Regulatory/compliance beyond durable, replayable storage
    • Network transport — how orders physically arrive

    Agree These First

    1 us to sequence, a 500 us total tickto-trade budget, a 1,000,000-event snapshot interval. Round, illustrative numbers, not any named exchange's.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Trading Client — Places, cancels, modifies — one of thousands, concurrently
    • (2) Gateway — Validates orders · forwards to the Sequencer — never assigns order itself
    • (8) Admission Control — Edge backpressure — sheds before the Sequencer's own queue can form
    • (3) Sequencer — The single writer — assigns a strict, monotonic sequence number to every event; 1 us to sequence + append · 1,000,000 events/s ceiling
    • (4) Event Log — Durable, append-only, strictly ordered — the sole source of truth; 1.728 TB/day · 100 B/event
    • (5) Matching Engine — Consumes the log in order · price-time priority — a pure function of the log
    • (6) Order Book — In-memory state — entirely rebuilt by replay, never its own source of truth
    • (7) Execution Report Publisher — Publishes trade confirmations and book updates to subscribers
    • (9) Snapshot Store — Periodic book snapshots — bounds recovery time
    • orders — avg 200,000/s · peak 600,000/s

    Data model:

    • events seq_no PK · type · payload · wall_clock_ts — reporting only
    • order_book derived, in-memory — rebuilt by replaying events, never its own truth

    Line = flow type: WRITE · order → log; CONFIRM · exec report; CHECK · snapshot.

    Say It Like This

    "This needs total order inside a budget that can't afford agreement between writers, so there's exactly one Sequencer — order is decided by arrival, not consensus. It hands out a strict, monotonic sequence number, and that number — never wall-clock time — is the one true ordering authority. Everything downstream is a pure function of replaying that log, which is why the log, not the book, is the source of truth."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) One writer, not consensus Order decided by arrival at one place — nothing to agree on A vote/consensus round costs far more than the latency budget allows
    (2) Gateway forwards, never assigns Only the Sequencer ever assigns order — one authority, one total order Every gateway assigning order → no single total order exists anywhere
    (4) The log is the truth, not the book Durable and replayable — everything else is rebuilt FROM it In-memory state is lost on crash, or drifts silently between replicas
    (5) Matching is its own stage Sequencing stays O(1) per event — its whole job is throughput Fusing matching in would throttle the entire pipeline behind it
    (6) Order Book is rebuildable, not stored Fully derived — rebuilt by replaying the log, on demand Persist it separately → a second, independently-drifting truth
    (8) Admission control, despite the headroom Sheds/throttles before the Sequencer's FIFO queue can form A burst queues and delays EVERY order behind it, not just the excess

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Budget tick-to-trade, Gateway to published report 500 us
    Ceiling 1 us to sequence + append, single-threaded 1,000,000 /s
    Avg load place + cancel + modify, baseline 200,000 /s
    Peak (×3) 3x average — tick-to-trade must still hold 600,000 /s
    Utilization 600,000 ÷ 1,000,000 ceiling — comfortable headroom 60%
    Stages Gateway 50 · Sequencing 1 · Log 50 · Matching 100 · Publish 100 301 us used

    Sequencing (1 us) is not the bottleneck — matching and publishing are, at 100 us apiece. 301 us of the 500 us budget is spoken for; 199 us of headroom is what the rest of the pipeline gets to spend.

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (8) A burst exceeds even Admission Control's throttling. Every order queued behind it inherits the growing latency — the Sequencer is strictly FIFO, single-threaded. — Fix: shed/reject excess at the edge — never let the queue grow unbounded
    • (9) A snapshot is inconsistent with a specific sequence number. Taken mid-write, recovery could apply the wrong prefix of the log or double-apply events. — Fix: tag every snapshot with the exact sequence number it reflects; replay only strictly after it

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Why not let a few gateways accept orders and agree on order afterward? (ref 3 — tests: any inter-writer coordination costs more than the budget allows)
    2. Why does the Gateway forward orders to the Sequencer instead of stamping order itself? (refs 2,3 — tests: multiple stampers means no single total order exists)
    3. The in-memory book already looks correct. Why is the Event Log still "the" truth? (ref 4 — tests: durability over a state that can silently diverge)
    4. Why isn't matching folded into the Sequencer's own hot path? (ref 5 — tests: sequencing must stay O(1) or it throttles the pipeline)
    5. Could you just persist the Order Book directly, like any other table? (ref 6 — tests: a second persisted copy is a second, driftable truth)
    6. The Sequencer has headroom to spare. Why do you still need Admission Control? (ref 8 — tests: a burst delays every order behind it, not just the excess)

    Cover The Sheet · Answer Aloud

    Use it as revision: cover the right rail and the two panels to your left, then walk the numbered path out loud. If you cannot say why a box is there and what breaks it, that box is your gap.

    Level 3 — The Edges (Advanced)

    Pacing: 10 min ask · 25 min trace flow · 15 min read why · 40 min cover & answer.

    The Ask

    Functional

    • Accept orders — place, cancel, modify
    • Assign each event a strict, monotonic sequence number
    • Match orders, price-time priority, from the sequenced log
    • Publish execution reports to subscribers
    • Recover from any failure, even the Sequencer, with no second writer

    Non-Functional

    • Tick-to-trade in low hundreds of us ← forces a single writer
    • Deterministic, auditable ordering ← forces sequence numbers
    • The book must never drift on its own ← forces event sourcing
    • Crash recovery can't replay from #1 ← forces periodic snapshots
    • No two live writers, ever ← forces fencing, not simple promotion

    Out of Scope — Say It Aloud

    • Regulatory/compliance beyond durable, replayable storage
    • Network transport — how orders physically arrive

    Agree These First

    1 us to sequence, a 500 us total tickto-trade budget, a 1,000,000-event snapshot interval. Round, illustrative numbers, not any named exchange's.

    The Shape

    Components and flow:

    • Trading Client — Places, cancels, modifies — one of thousands, concurrently
    • (2) Gateway — Validates orders · forwards to the Sequencer — never assigns order itself
    • (8) Admission Control — Edge backpressure — sheds before the Sequencer's own queue can form
    • (3) Sequencer — The single writer — assigns a strict, monotonic sequence number to every event; 1 us to sequence + append · 1,000,000 events/s ceiling
    • (11) Sequencer Failover / Fencing — Proves the old Sequencer can never write again — before a backup starts
    • (4) Event Log — Durable, append-only, strictly ordered — the sole source of truth; 1.728 TB/day · 100 B/event
    • (5) Matching Engine — Consumes the log in order · price-time priority — a pure function of the log
    • (10) Standby Matching Engine — Consumes the SAME log in parallel — identical state, no replication protocol
    • (6) Order Book — In-memory state — entirely rebuilt by replay, never its own source of truth
    • (7) Execution Report Publisher — Publishes trade confirmations and book updates to subscribers
    • (9) Snapshot Store — Periodic book snapshots — bounds recovery time
    • (12) Audit / Replay Tool — Reconstructs any point-intime state straight from the durable log
    • (13) Timestamp Authority — Wall-clock for reporting only — the sequence number is the true order
    • orders — avg 200,000/s · peak 600,000/s

    Data model:

    • events seq_no PK · type · payload · wall_clock_ts — reporting only
    • order_book derived, in-memory — rebuilt by replaying events, never its own truth

    Line = flow type: WRITE · order → log; CONFIRM · exec report; CHECK · snapshot; BYPASS · reads ④.

    Say It Like This

    "This needs total order inside a budget that can't afford agreement between writers, so there's exactly one Sequencer — order is decided by arrival, not consensus. It hands out a strict, monotonic sequence number, and that number — never wall-clock time — is the one true ordering authority. Everything downstream is a pure function of replaying that log, which is why the log, not the book, is the source of truth."

    Defend It

    Decision Prefer Avoid
    (3) One writer, not consensus Order decided by arrival at one place — nothing to agree on A vote/consensus round costs far more than the latency budget
    (2) Gateway forwards, never assigns Only the Sequencer ever assigns order — one authority, one total order Every gateway assigning order → no single total order exists anywhere
    (4) The log is the truth, not the book Durable and replayable — everything else is rebuilt FROM it In-memory state is lost on crash, or drifts silently between replicas
    (5) Matching is its own stage Sequencing stays O(1) per event — its whole job is throughput Fusing matching in would throttle the entire pipeline behind it
    (6) Order Book is rebuildable, not stored Fully derived — rebuilt by replaying the log, on demand Persist it separately → a second, independently-drifting truth
    (8) Admission control, despite the headroom Sheds/throttles before the Sequencer's FIFO queue can form A burst queues and delays EVERY order behind it, not just the excess
    (10) No replication protocol needed Same log, same order in → identical state out, guaranteed A bespoke replication protocol is more machinery that can itself diverge
    (11) Fencing, not simple promotion Proves the old Sequencer is provably dead before a backup A "dead" primary that's really just slow could still be alive and writing

    Capacity Math

    Quantity Derivation Value
    Budget tick-to-trade, Gateway to published report 500 us
    Ceiling 1 us to sequence + append, single-threaded 1,000,000 /s
    Avg load place + cancel + modify, baseline 200,000 /s
    Peak (×3) 3x average — tick-to-trade must still hold 600,000 /s
    Utilization 600,000 ÷ 1,000,000 ceiling — comfortable headroom 60%
    Stages Gateway 50 · Sequencing 1 · Log 50 · Matching 100 · Publish 100 301 us used
    Headroom 500 us budget − 301 us used 199 us
    Log growth 200,000/s × 86,400 s/day = 17,280,000,000/day × 100 B 1.728 TB/day
    Recovery 1,000,000-event snapshots, replayed at 500,000 events/s 2.0 s

    1.728 TB/day is not the hard part — a single-threaded sequencer producing it at 60% of its ceiling is. The 2.0 s worst-case recovery is what a periodic snapshot buys: a bounded number, not "replay everything since sequence #1."

    What Breaks — Say It Before They Ask

    • (8) A burst exceeds even Admission Control's throttling. Every order queued behind it inherits the growing latency — the Sequencer is strictly FIFO, single-threaded. — Fix: shed/reject excess at the edge — never let the queue grow unbounded
    • (9) A snapshot is inconsistent with a specific sequence number. Taken mid-write, recovery could apply the wrong prefix of the log or double-apply events. — Fix: tag every snapshot with the exact sequence number it reflects; replay only strictly after it
    • (3) The Sequencer itself crashes. Without fencing, a naive failover risks two sequencers briefly both accepting writes — two divergent total orders. — Fix: fencing must guarantee the old one is provably dead before the backup ever writes
    • (10) The Standby Matching Engine falls behind. Slow disk, CPU contention. Its book is stale relative to the primary's. — Fix: never promote it to serve or match until fully caught up to the primary's last sequence number
    • (13) Wall-clock time gets mistaken for the ordering authority. A report tool sorts by timestamp instead of sequence number, and clocks can skew or jump out of true order. — Fix: only the sequence number is the true order, ever — timestamps are for reporting alone

    The Follow-Ups That Decide It

    1. Why not let a few gateways accept orders and agree on order afterward? (ref 3 — tests: any inter-writer coordination costs more than the budget allows)
    2. Why does the Gateway forward orders to the Sequencer instead of stamping order itself? (refs 2,3 — tests: multiple stampers means no single total order exists)
    3. The in-memory book already looks correct. Why is the Event Log still "the" truth? (ref 4 — tests: durability over a state that can silently diverge)
    4. Why isn't matching folded into the Sequencer's own hot path? (ref 5 — tests: sequencing must stay O(1) or it throttles the pipeline)
    5. Could you just persist the Order Book directly, like any other table? (ref 6 — tests: a second persisted copy is a second, driftable truth)
    6. The Sequencer has headroom to spare. Why do you still need Admission Control? (ref 8 — tests: a burst delays every order behind it, not just the excess)
    7. How does the standby matching engine stay in sync with no replication protocol? (ref 10 — tests: replaying the same log to the same point IS the replication)
    8. Your Sequencer stops responding. Why not just promote the backup right away? (ref 11 — tests: an unfenced "dead" primary might still be alive and writing)
