    Agents and tool use

    1 sections · 518 words

    Module 09 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Defend an autonomous loop past the happy path.

    Everyone who has built a demo says they built an agent. This round checks whether the loop ever touched a real system with a real failure mode: a tool that came back with a 500, a step count nobody capped, a subagent that duplicated work because it never saw its sibling's reasoning. A candidate who narrates the happy path fluently, and has nothing to say about step seven when the loop will not converge, is exactly who this module is built to catch.

    Inside this module: Tool schemas · Loop control · State & replay · Trajectory eval

    Concept checkpoints

    Q9.1 — Walk me through what's actually different between this and three LLM calls chained in a fixed order.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can name the model choosing its own next action, not just "an LLM is involved" or "it runs unattended".

    Q9.2 — How did you define your agent's tools, and what does the model actually see for each one?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows the model sees only a name, a description and a schema, never source code, and that description quality drives selection.

    Q9.3 — Describe the loop your agent actually runs. What happens each iteration, and what stops it?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a concrete plan-act-observe loop with an enforced termination, not just "it stops when it's done".

    Q9.4 — On step ten, what does the agent actually have access to, and where does that come from?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate separates in-context history from state persisted outside the run, and what a crash does to each.

    Q9.5 — Your agent can issue a refund directly. What happens on failure, and why no human first?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate designs the failure path as carefully as the happy path, with a real reversibility rule for human checkpoints.

    Q9.6 — A per-file step cap runs across 3,000 files. What stops a runaway bill, and where does that cap alone fail?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a second control beyond the per-run cap: a cost ceiling, a circuit breaker, and a human kill switch.

    Q9.7 — You'd split this into an orchestrator plus a subagent per table. Convince me that beats one agent alone.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate weighs real coordination costs of a multi-agent split, not just parallel speed.

    Q9.8 — The migration passed the test suite. Convince me you'd still catch a bad run, or admit you wouldn't.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate checks tool-selection accuracy and step correctness, not just whether the final output passed.

    Q9.9 — [DRILL] Specify an invoice-matching agent: the tools, what stops the loop, and every failure path.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can assemble a full agent design in one pass: scoped tools, a real termination, and failure handling.
