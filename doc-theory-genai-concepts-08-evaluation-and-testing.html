    Evaluation and testing

    1 sections · 700 words

    Module 08 · 13 questions · 1 practical drill

    Measure a system that answers differently every time.

    The gap this round finds: candidates who ran a notebook once, eyeballed a dozen outputs and called it evaluation, against candidates who can describe an actual measurement discipline. A golden set rebuilt from real traffic. A judge checked against a human before anyone trusted it. An online number that isn't secretly the same as the offline one. This module sits before agents, because you cannot responsibly hand more autonomy to a system you cannot yet measure. Follow-ups punish a cherry-picked before and after, a judge score nobody validated, and an evaluation process that quietly costs more than anyone priced it at.

    Inside this module: Golden sets · LLM-as-judge · Judge calibration · Eval cost

    How to work this module: alternative you rejected, and what actually failed. Those three are what the follow-ups are hunting for.

    Concept checkpoints

    Q8.1 — You changed a prompt, model or retrieval step. How do you actually know it made things better?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a repeatable before and after process, not a handful of manual spot checks that change every time.

    Q8.2 — How do you build a golden set for this feature, and keep it useful over time?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate builds a representative, labelled, versioned set from real usage, and knows it decays without upkeep.

    Q8.3 — Tell me about using an LLM to judge your own model's outputs. Where does that go wrong?

    What it really tests: Real depth on judge bias and rigour, not just "we used a model to score outputs" with nothing behind it.

    Q8.4 — What's the difference between evaluating this offline and what you measure once it's live?

    What it really tests: Whether offline and online evaluation are understood as genuinely different tools, not two names for the same check.

    Q8.5 — The model gives a different answer every run. How do you regression test that in CI?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate tests properties under sampling variance, not a single exact string diff pretending determinism away.

    Q8.6 — How do you catch hallucination once this is live, without a labelled correct answer to check?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has production viable detection signals, not just notebook techniques too costly to run on every request.

    Q8.7 — This task has no single correct answer, summaries, support replies. How do you pick metrics?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate decomposes quality into task specific, checkable dimensions, not one generic score.

    Q8.8 — What does running your evals actually cost, and how much evaluation is enough?

    What it really tests: Whether evaluation is treated as a real, decomposable cost, judge tokens, labelling hours, CI time, with a return.

    Q8.9 — [DRILL] Design the eval suite for a support feature launching in two weeks.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate assembles golden set construction, judge validation, the offline and online split, and cost into one plan.

    Q8.10 — How do you actually check that your LLM judge agrees with what a human reviewer would say?

    What it really tests: Whether judge quality is validated against a labelled human sample, or simply assumed because the judge sounds confident.

    Q8.11 — How do you measure whether two human labellers actually agree, and what do you do when they don't?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats low agreement as a rubric problem to fix, or as a reason to just pick one labeller's view.

    Q8.12 — Your eval set says one run scored 3 points higher than another. How do you know that's real?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has any real sense of sample size and noise, or treats any higher number as a genuine win.

    Q8.13 — The output is different every run. How do you set a threshold that actually catches a real regression?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate sets a data-driven pass-rate threshold, or expects a single deterministic pass or fail.
