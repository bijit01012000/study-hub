    Security, privacy and safety

    1 sections · 538 words

    Module 13 · 9 questions · 1 practical drill

    Threat-model a system where untrusted text meets privileged instructions.

    Every other module in this guide assumes the content flowing through the model is something the user typed, or something you deliberately put there. This module is about the moment that stops being true, when a document, an email or another tenant's data enters the same context window as the instructions the system follows, and the model cannot reliably tell the two apart. Almost every candidate has heard of prompt injection. The follow-ups here ask what happens next, because naming the vulnerability and then proposing a disclaimer and a phrase blocklist as the whole defence demonstrates the exact gap this module probes for.

    Inside this module: Prompt injection · Blast radius · Tenant isolation · Retention

    How to work this module: Answer each question out loud in 90 seconds before reading the model answer. Then check you named a number, the alternative you rejected, and what actually failed. Those three are what the follow-ups are hunting for.

    Concept checkpoints

    Q13.1 — An email assistant drafts replies and forwards messages. How do you defend it against injection?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats injection as architectural to contain, not prose to patch, and knows direct from indirect unprompted.

    Q13.2 — Your RAG wiki assistant puts retrieved documents into context. What's entering, and what do you do?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats context as one undifferentiated channel, and retrieved content as adversarial by default.

    Q13.3 — A code-review bot can comment on PRs. It's asked to auto-apply small fixes. How do you scope it?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate reasons about permission scoping and blast radius as the real control against a bad decision.

    Q13.4 — An on-call assistant sends production logs to a third-party LLM API. What stops PII leaving?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has mapped what crosses the boundary, versus treating a data agreement as a technical control.

    Q13.5 — One shared vector index is filtered by tenant_id at query time. Walk me through what could go wrong.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows metadata filtering is a convenience, not a security boundary, unless enforced server-side.

    Q13.6 — An ops assistant proposes a remediation action. Before it runs, what actually validates it?

    What it really tests: Whether an independent validation step exists between model output and execution, or the model is simply trusted.

    Q13.7 — A guardrail model catches unsafe output for your shopping assistant. Where does it break?

    What it really tests: Whether guardrails are understood as a probabilistic, bypassable layer, not a solved problem once installed.

    Q13.8 — Your support assistant logs full prompts with names and account numbers. What's your retention policy?

    What it really tests: Whether prompt and response logs are treated as regulated personal data, not debugging exhaust kept forever.

    Q13.9 — [DRILL] An agent ingests documents and admins can trigger re-index or delete by name. Threat-model it.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can threat-model an agent across content, action, tenancy and boundary risks systematically.
