      Outbox Pattern & Dual-Write

      0 sections · 379 words

      30. What's the dual-write problem?

      When one operation must write to two systems — the DB and Kafka — they're independent I/O calls, so any failure between them causes divergence. DB commits but Kafka is down → the event is lost forever and downstream never finds out. Or Kafka publishes but the DB rolls back → you announced a change that doesn't exist. There's no way to make two separate systems atomic with a plain try/catch.

      31. How does the outbox pattern fix it?

      Write the business entity and an outbox_events row in the same DB transaction. Both commit or both roll back — you can never have a committed business change without its pending event. A separate scheduled poller reads unpublished rows, sends them to Kafka, and marks them published. The Kafka publish becomes a consequence of a committed row, not a competing write.

      32. Show me where this happens in code.

      In TicketService.createTicket — inside one @Transactional method I ticketRepository.save(ticket) and outboxEventRepository.save(new OutboxEvent(...)). Same for status changes, chat escalation (ChatService.handleEscalation), and IngestionService.completeIngestion (document status → COMPLETED + document.ingested outbox row, atomically).

      33. What does the poller guarantee — exactly-once?

      At-least-once. OutboxEventPoller runs on a schedule, reads unpublished rows, publishes, marks published. If it crashes after publishing but before marking published, it republishes on restart — a duplicate. So the delivery guarantee is at-least-once, and consumers must be idempotent to get effectively-once.

      34. Why two separate tables — outbox_events and ticket_events?

      Different purposes, retention, and query patterns. outbox_events is a delivery mechanism — write once, mark published, lifecycle ends at Kafka. ticket_events is an audit trail — read by agents, shown in ticket history, kept indefinitely. Merging them would put a meaningless published column on your audit log and make your outbox queries scan audit-only rows.

      35. How does the poller behave once you actually wire Kafka?

      Early phases the poller just logged the event and marked it published — proving the transaction semantics without a broker. Wiring Kafka replaced the log line with kafkaTemplate.send(eventType, aggregateId, payload). Everything else — scheduling, the unpublished query, mark-published, at-least-once retry — stayed identical. That's the payoff of building the outbox structure first: Kafka is a config swap, not a rewrite.
