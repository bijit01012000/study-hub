    Model selection and adaptation

    1 sections · 691 words

    Module 04 · 13 questions · 1 practical drill

    Pick a model on evidence you gathered, not a leaderboard.

    This is the round where the interviewer checks whether you can actually choose and adapt a model, not just call one. Base, instruct, reasoning, quantised, hosted, open-weight, fine-tuned: these all sound like implementation detail until you have shipped a feature with a real cost, latency and privacy budget, and then they are the whole decision. Most candidates have a strong opinion about which model is best and no method for finding out. What this module checks is narrower: can you build a private evaluation set instead of trusting a leaderboard, can you say plainly what a fine-tune actually teaches and what it does not, and can you treat a model swap with the same discipline as any other production migration.

    Inside this module: Private evals · Hosted vs open · Fine-tuning data · Migration

    How to work this module: Answer each question out loud in 90 seconds before reading the model answer. Then check you named a number, the alternative you rejected, and what actually failed. Those three are what the follow-ups are hunting for.

    Concept checkpoints

    Q4.1 — Base model, instruction-tuned model, reasoning model: what changes for you as an engineer?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can pick a model class on cost and latency grounds, not just capability, and knows where the stronger class loses.

    Q4.2 — What does quantising a model to INT8 or FP16 for inference actually cost you?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate understands quantisation as a precision-for-speed trade with a real accuracy cost, not a free win.

    Q4.3 — Prompting, retrieval, fine-tuning: how do you actually decide between them?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has a real decision boundary based on the type of gap, rather than a fixed escalation ladder.

    Q4.4 — What are LoRA and QLoRA actually doing when you fine-tune with them?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can explain the mechanism at application-engineer depth, not just recite the efficiency benefit.

    Q4.5 — You're picking a model for a new production feature. Walk me through how.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate weighs private eval results, cost, latency and operational factors, not just capability or reputation.

    Q4.6 — Why aren't public benchmark scores enough to choose a model for your feature?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows benchmarks are commonly contaminated and do not reflect the actual production task distribution.

    Q4.7 — Hosted model against open-weight model: how do you actually decide?

    What it really tests: Whether the trade-off is reasoned through operational burden, privacy and cost at volume, not treated as ideology.

    Q4.8 — How would you build a model gateway without papering over real provider differences?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate can design an abstraction that helps swapping models without hiding the behaviour that actually differs.

    Q4.9 — How do you actually build a fine-tuning dataset that's worth training on?

    What it really tests: Whether quality, diversity and deduplication are prioritised over raw size, and eval data is known to be kept separate.

    Q4.10 — How do you make sure your fine-tuning data hasn't contaminated your evaluation set?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate has an actual mechanism for keeping eval data separate, not just an intention to be careful.

    Q4.11 — Supervised fine-tuning against preference optimisation: what's the actual difference?

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate knows SFT teaches from examples of correct output while preference methods teach from comparisons.

    Q4.12 — Your fine-tune improved the target task but broke something else. What happened?

    What it really tests: Whether catastrophic forgetting and narrow training data are understood as the mechanism, not treated as a mystery.

    Q4.13 — Drill: migrate a live feature to a different model with no visible regression.

    What it really tests: Whether the candidate treats a model swap as a proper migration, not a config edit, with its own evaluation and rollback plan.
