    Design a Splitwise

    15 sections · 2,024 words

    LLD Design Problems, problem 13 of 20.

    The Ask

    Build

    • Users, and a group of users.
    • An expense: a payer, an integer total, a split across participants.
    • 4 split types: EQUAL, EXACT, PERCENT, SHARES.
    • Every split's shares sum to the exact total.
    • A net balance per user, and getBalances(user).
    • Simplify debts: the fewest transfers to settle up.
    • Add, edit, delete an expense; edit reverses then reapplies.
    • Settle-up records a payment.

    Skip

    • Real payment or settlement rails.
    • Currency conversion detail.
    • Receipt OCR, itemised bills.
    • A notifications service.
    • Friend requests, the social graph.
    • A database, login.

    Our Model

    A member adds and settles. An ExpenseService owns groups, a net map and the expense log. Each expense carries a SplitType; a SplitStrategy turns the total into shares that sum back to it.

    Actors and classes

    • Member — id, name, svc: ExpenseService. Chooses the SplitStrategy.
    • Group — id, name, members: List<String>, feed: ActivityLog; addMember(String user), expenses(): List<Expense>. The feed is the Observer: every add notifies.
    • ExpenseService (singleton) — groups: Map<String, Group>, net: Map<String, Integer> (paise), log: List<Expense> (source of truth), split: SplitStrategy, rounding: RoundingPolicy; addExpense(Expense e), split(SplitType t, int total, List<Participant> parts): Map<String, Integer>, getBalances(String user): Map<String, Integer>, simplify(Map<String, Integer> net): List<Transfer>, plus editExpense, deleteExpense, settleUp. Every write goes under one balance-sheet lock.
    • Expense — id, payer: String, total: int, type: SplitType, participants: List<Participant>, shares: Map<String, Integer>, deltas: Map<String, Integer> (kept for reverse); shareOf(String u): int; apply() / reverse() run through ExpenseCommand. Shares always sum to total.
    • ExpenseCommand (command) — expense: Expense, deltas: Map<String, Integer>; apply(), reverse(). Edit = reverse the old, apply the new.
    • SplitStrategy (interface) — split(int total, List<Participant> parts): Map<String, Integer>. A total in, shares that sum back to it out. The remainder lands on one person, by RoundingPolicy.
    • EqualSplit / ExactSplit / PercentSplit / SharesSplit — the four concrete strategies.
    • RoundingPolicy (interface) — absorb(int remainder, List<String> users): String. Picks who eats the leftover paise: LastParticipant (default), Payer, RoundRobin.
    • BalanceSheet — net: Map<String, Integer> (paise); applyDelta(String u, int d), netOf(String u): int. The sum of every net is always 0.
    • Participant (value) — user: String, value: int. The per-person amount, percent or weight for non-EQUAL splits.
    • Transfer (value) — from, to: String, amount: int. One settle-up payment; the output of simplify.
    • Money (value) — amount: long, minor units. Integer paise, never a float.
    • SplitType (enum) — EQUAL, EXACT, PERCENT, SHARES.

    The four split rules

    • EqualSplit -> total / n, last takes the remainder.
    • ExactSplit -> given amounts, must sum to total.
    • PercentSplit -> percents sum to 100, then apply.
    • SharesSplit -> weights, total x w / sum(w), last absorbs.

    Key operations

    1. addExpense(Expense e): void — splits the total through the expense's SplitType, asserts the shares sum to the exact total, then moves each share from the payer's credit to that participant's debt on the net map. Appended to the group log.
    2. split(SplitType t, int total, List<Participant> parts): Map<String, Integer> — dispatches to the strategy for t. EQUAL divides and hands the last user the remainder; EXACT and PERCENT reject inputs that do not add up. Always returns shares that total total.
    3. getBalances(String user): Map<String, Integer> — every other user mapped to what they owe this user; negative means this user owes them. Read only, derived from the net map, mutates nothing.
    4. simplify(Map<String, Integer> net): List<Transfer> — greedy: match the largest creditor with the largest debtor, settle the smaller magnitude, repeat. At most n - 1 transfers, never a self-transfer.

    Watching the message traffic

    • addExpense -> split -> net map deltas -> group log append.
    • getBalances -> read net map, no mutation.
    • simplify -> credential/debt heaps -> transfer list.
    • editExpense -> reverse old deltas, apply new.
    • settleUp -> a Transfer, one net delta each way.
    • All splits must satisfy: shares always sum to total.

    Recall drill: cover the operations column, write the four with their guarantee. Most people forget that split() has to make the shares sum to the total, to the paisa.

    The One Run to Hold in Your Head

    A group of four. Dinner is Rs 4,000, Aisha paid, split EQUAL. Then Bob adds Rs 1,200 for the cab, split by shares 2:1:1. What does getBalances show for Aisha, and which transfers settle the group?

    The invariant: Rs 100.00 split EQUAL three ways is 33.33 + 33.33 + 33.34 = 100.00; the last absorbs +0.01. EXACT, PERCENT and SHARES validate first, then use the same absorb rule.

    The Change

    They want a different netting algorithm — min cash flow, settle only within circles, or simplification switched off. Pull the greedy loop out of the service into a SimplifyStrategy that takes the net map and returns the transfer list. The service just calls it. Strategy, a second time on this sheet.

    What the Picture Can't Show

    The concurrent edit. Two people change one expense at once, both reverse the old deltas, the new deltas get applied twice and the group is off by a whole expense. The fix is not a class, it is a lock per balance sheet with reverse-then-reapply as one step.

    If They Make You Code It

    Add expense and simplify, the whole thing:

    class ExpenseService {                                   // one instance
    Map<String,Integer> net = new ConcurrentHashMap<>(); // paise
    List<Expense> log = new ArrayList<>();

    void addExpense(Expense e) {
        Map<String,Integer> share =
            split(e.type(), e.total(), e.participants());
        int sum = 0;
        for (int v : share.values()) sum += v;
        if (sum != e.total())                            // never drop a paisa
            throw new SplitMismatch(sum, e.total());
        synchronized (net) {                             // one edit at a time
            for (Participant p : e.participants()) {
                String u = p.user();
                if (u.equals(e.payer())) continue;
                int s = share.get(u);
                net.merge(e.payer(), s, Integer::sum);   // is owed
                net.merge(u, -s, Integer::sum);          // owes
            }
        }
        e.setDeltas(share);                              // kept, so edit reverses
        log.add(e);
    }

    List<Transfer> simplify(Map<String,Integer> net) {
        PriorityQueue<Bal> cred = maxHeap(), debt = maxHeap();
        net.forEach((u,v) -> { if (v != 0)               // skip settled
            (v > 0 ? cred : debt).add(new Bal(u, Math.abs(v))); });
        List<Transfer> out = new ArrayList<>();
        while (!cred.isEmpty() && !debt.isEmpty()) {
            Bal c = cred.poll(), d = debt.poll();
            int x = Math.min(c.amt, d.amt);              // smaller side
            out.add(new Transfer(d.user, c.user, x));
            if (c.amt > x) cred.add(new Bal(c.user, c.amt - x));
            if (d.amt > x) debt.add(new Bal(d.user, d.amt - x));
        }
        return out;                                      // <= n-1 transfers
    }
}

    The Four Hazards

    1. Shares don't add up to the total. Rs 100 split three ways is 33.33 each, which is 99.99. Rounding each share loses or gains a paisa. Give the whole remainder to one deterministic person, the last participant, never spread it silently or drop it.
    2. An edit that doesn't reverse itself. Editing an expense has to undo the exact deltas it made, then apply the new ones. Recompute the shares from scratch and you subtract the wrong numbers. Keep the deltas on the Expense; edit = reverse those then apply the new; or rebuild the whole log.
    3. Money as a double. Rs 0.1 + Rs 0.2 is not Rs 0.3. Floating point makes every balance drift a little further with each expense. Integer paise everywhere, format to rupees only at the very edge.
    4. Simplify emits a bad transfer. A greedy step that pairs a user with themselves, or leaves a cycle, means the netting is wrong. Clear one side fully each step so no cycle forms, and skip any transfer where from == to.

    Name them before they are asked.

    The Three Lines They Are Watching

    1. if (sum != e.total()) — the shares must total exactly; the remainder is placed in split(), never dropped here.
    2. synchronized (net) + merge — one add or edit at a time, so nothing double-counts.
    3. Math.min(c.amt, d.amt) then push the rest back — each loop clears one side, so it ends in at most n - 1 transfers.

    Where Balances Live

    Approach "Does A owe B?" Space Add expense Edit expense Best when
    Net per user (the default) derive via simplify O(n) n merges reverse + reapply deltas who owes whom overall
    Per-pair debts direct read O(n^2) pairs touch each pair adjust the pairs show direct debts
    Recompute from the log replay every expense O(expenses) append only rebuild from zero audit, correctness

    Net per user. O(n) space, and getBalances plus simplify answer everything an interviewer asks. Keep the expense log as the source of truth so any edit can be rebuilt. Add a per-pair map only when the product must show "you owe Bob Rs 200" without simplifying first.

    Rejection Signals

    These end the interview:

    • Shares that don't add up to the total — a paisa quietly dropped or spread. Every split has to reconcile exactly.
    • Money as a double — paise stop summing and every balance drifts.
    • An expense that mutates balances with no stored deltas — a wrong edit can never be reversed.
    • Balances kept only as per-pair debts, no net view — simplify has nothing to run on.
    • A simplify that emits a self-transfer or loops forever — the greedy match is not thought through.
    • Dropping a user from the group while they still carry a non-zero balance — the debt just vanishes.
    • Branching on the split type inside addExpense: if (type == PERCENT) ladders. Put it behind split(type, total, users) so EQUAL, EXACT, PERCENT and SHARES all drop in.

    Patterns Used

    Strategy — the four split types behind one split(). Command — an Expense that applies and reverses, so edit = reverse + re-apply. Observer — the group activity feed follows every add. Factory — build the SplitStrategy from a SplitType.

    Say It Like This

    "An expense is a payer, an integer total in paise, and a SplitType. A SplitStrategy turns the total into shares that sum to it exactly, the last person absorbs the rounding. addExpense moves each share from the payer's credit to that user's debt on a net-per-user map. simplify greedily matches the biggest creditor with the biggest debtor, at most n-1 transfers. An edit reverses the stored deltas, then re-applies."

    The Follow-ups That Decide It

    1. Rs 100 three ways — who gets the extra paisa? The last participant, deterministically, inside split().
    2. Someone edits an old expense. Reverse the stored deltas, then apply the new expense.
    3. Show who owes whom in the fewest payments. simplify: greedy biggest creditor vs biggest debtor, at most n - 1.
    4. EXACT split where the amounts don't total. split() throws, nothing touches the balances.
    5. A member joins after expenses exist. They owe nothing from before; only new expenses include them.
    6. Two people add to one group at once. Lock the net map or the group, apply each fully.
    7. Split by shares, 2:1:1 on Rs 400. Weights to 200 / 100 / 100, last absorbs any remainder.
    8. Settle up — I pay Bob Rs 500. A Transfer, one net delta each way, appended like an expense.

    Deliberately Not Built

    • Real settlement rails — a PSP behind a thin Payment seam. settleUp only records that money moved.
    • Currency conversion — one currency per group here. Multi-currency is the ghost on page 1.
    • Receipt OCR — scanning a bill into line items. A different question.
    • Friend graph — requests, blocks, contacts. Assume the group is given.
    • Notifications — email and push on every change. Name it and move on.
